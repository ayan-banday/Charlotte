# substack-note-writer

Transform Ash's raw thoughts into Substack notes. A note is a short standalone post read cold, in a feed, by a stranger who has never read Ash before and owes him nothing. Every rule in this file exists because that stranger gives the first line about two seconds.

This skill was built from a scored session (2026-07-19): every law below carries a real failed draft and a real passed draft from that session. The laws are the gate. A draft that breaks one never reaches Ash.

**Voice:** inherit `ash-substack-voice` fully (peer voice, vocabulary, sentence fingerprint, metaphor domains, anti-patterns). This file adds the note-specific layer on top. Where the two conflict, this file wins for notes.

---

## THE CORE TRANSFORM

Ash's raw thoughts are first person conclusions about his own life. They feel dead to him because he already lived the tension that produced them. The conversion:

1. The tension he lived gets restated as **a law the reader is currently breaking.**
2. The POV flips from "I keep doing this" to "you do this."
3. His lived specifics stay in as **proof**, placed after the claim.

An idea is not interesting or uninteresting. It is accusatory and specific, or it is neither.

---

## THE DEFAULT SPINE

Claim → Evidence → Escalation. Default, not straitjacket: a swipe-file shape may override it (Verdict has no evidence block, Quote + Endorsement has no claim of Ash's). When in doubt, use the spine.

- **Line 1 — the claim.** Arguable, subject named in plain words, no setup. A stranger must be able to disagree with it. Story never opens a note: a newsletter has a headline and a subscribe decision doing trust-work before the first line. A note has nothing. Claim first is how trust gets earned.
- **Middle — the evidence.** One real scene from Ash's life with one falsifiable detail. This is where "I" enters.
- **Close — the escalation.** A line that goes PAST the opener: adds a mechanism, a cost, or an inversion. Restating the opener in different words is a failed close.

The two notes Ash rated highest both had: a real lived scene + a mechanism for why the trap works + a close that inverted something. That is the quality bar, not just law compliance.

---

## THE SIX STEPS

Run these on every raw thought, in order.

1. **Strip to the claim.** One sentence: what does Ash actually believe here?
2. **Find the tension.** What did it cost, what was the gap between intent and action? A conclusion with its conflict removed is a platitude. This step is what makes the note worth a stranger's time.
3. **Flip the POV.** First person conclusion → second person law. Keep "I" only for the evidence block.
4. **Pick the shape.** Test the thought against the nine shapes below. Draft the 2 or 3 that fit.
5. **Anchor it.** One concrete detail from the raw material. Real, falsifiable, slightly strange. If none exists: ASK or leave a marked blank. Never fabricate (Law 8).
6. **Cut until it stings.** Delete setup, delete hedges, make the last line land.

## THE NINE SHAPES

Derived from `05 Notes and Ideas/Swipe Files/Substack Notes Swipe.md`. One example each, from that file.

| Shape | Mechanic | Swipe example |
|---|---|---|
| **Verdict** | One line, no support. Must carry a second half that works: instruction + consequence, accusation + specific object, mechanism + condition. Never a bare diagnosis. | "learn to disappoint people or they'll design your life for you" (04) |
| **Named Trap** | Name the collective delusion, then hand over the real thing. | "There is a sickness going around where we believe the ultimate goal is a million dollars..." (01) |
| **Physical Analogy Attack** | Give an abstract harm a body the reader can see. Highest-yield shape in the file. | "You wouldn't let 100 random people break into your house... Yet you willingly do that with your mind." (13) |
| **Math as Revelation** | Recompute a familiar number until it is unrecognisable. | "2-4 hours you spend scrolling each day (or 730-1460 hours each year)" (11) |
| **Confession-Law** | Personal cost buys the right to state a law. Law lands inside the same breath as the confession. | "It took me too many years to learn this, but you have to develop the ability to be disliked..." (17) |
| **List with Kill Line** | The list is filler; the line after it is the note. List items are things or actions, never qualities. | "How to master any skill fast... Most people don't get past 1." (12) |
| **Objection-Rebuttal** | Quote the reader's excuse in their own voice, then demolish it. | "'I'm not a creative person.' No, you are, everyone is, but..." (20) |
| **Contrast Pair** | Two short blocks: X does this, Y does that. | "Most social media makes you want to keep scrolling. After a few minutes on Substack..." (08) |
| **Quote + Endorsement** | Someone else's line plus one line of Ash. Cheapest note that exists; roughly one per week from reading. | "I think this is spot on: 'If you're going to use an LLM to write me an email...'" (09) |

Shape audit: quarterly, against the swipe file. A shape with zero accepted notes in 8 weeks gets cut.

## THE THIRTEEN LAWS

Every law in the same format: what it is, what breaking it looks like, why that fails, what obeying it looks like, why that works. All examples are real drafts from the build session. These run as a **gate**: check every draft against all thirteen before it reaches Ash. Fix or kill failures silently.

---

**Law 1. The camera test.**
Every noun must be something a camera could film. Private vocabulary gets translated or cut.
- **Violation:** "I spent a week building sourcebooks for a subject I never studied."
- **Why it fails:** "Sourcebook" is a word from inside Ash's head. A stranger stops to decode instead of feeling accused. Jargon is compression, and compression only works for people who already have the file. (Also unusable raw: open loop, dysregulate, pre-study, Man of Zero, RGS, the Merchant.)
- **Correct:** "I spent a week making notes for a subject I never studied."
- **Why it works:** The camera sees a kid making notes. Nothing to decode, nowhere to hide.

**Law 2. No negative parallelism.**
No "not X, but Y." No "it's not this, it's that." No "you are not X, you are Y." State the point positively.
- **Violation:** "You are not lazy. You just prefer the part of the work that cannot fail."
- **Why it fails:** Named AI tell (Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing), already banned in ash-substack-voice, and structurally lazy: the negation does no work.
- **Correct:** "Start before you are ready or you will spend your whole life getting ready."
- **Why it works:** Instruction, then the consequence of ignoring it. Same shape as swipe 04. Both halves carry weight.

**Law 3. One detail, never three.**
One anchor per scene. Lists are of things or actions. A list of adjectives is always AI.
- **Violation:** "Colour coded, neatly indexed, genuinely beautiful."
- **Why it fails:** Rule of three, the most recognisable AI rhythm. All three items are decorative; remove any and nothing changes. Swipe-file lists (01, 12) list things and actions, never qualities.
- **Correct:** "I bought new pens for it."
- **Why it works:** One detail, load bearing, faintly humiliating. A human writing from memory reaches for one detail, and it is usually slightly strange, because it is real.

**Law 4. No naming without mechanism.**
A pattern is only worth stating with the cost, the consequence, or the reason attached.
- **Violation:** "You just prefer the part of the work that cannot fail." (full stop, nothing after)
- **Why it fails:** Names a pattern and stops. No mechanism, no cost. Reads like a diagnosis from someone who never did the thing.
- **Correct:** "Getting ready cannot fail. That is the whole appeal. Nothing you make while getting ready will ever tell you that you are not good enough yet. Opening the book does that in about four minutes."
- **Why it works:** The WHY the trap works is on the page, with a body and a clock.

**Law 5. No inflated significance verbs.**
Banned: serves as, stands as, underscores, highlights, reflects a broader, is a testament to, marks a pivotal. Use plain copulas.
- **Violation:** "This serves as a reminder that preparation is a trap."
- **Why it fails:** Wikipedia-listed AI register. The verb claims importance instead of demonstrating it.
- **Correct:** "Preparation is a trap."
- **Why it works:** "Is" forces the claim to stand on its own.

**Law 6. No decorative intensifiers.**
Genuinely, truly, really, deeply, incredibly: cut on sight.
- **Violation:** "genuinely beautiful"
- **Why it fails:** The intensifier props up a word that cannot hold weight anyway. Doubt, dressed as emphasis.
- **Correct:** Cut the phrase or replace with a filmable detail.
- **Why it works:** If the noun needs propping, the noun is wrong.

**Law 7. Straight quotes, no em dashes.**
Periods, commas, colons. Straight apostrophes and quotes.
- **Violation:** "Being misunderstood, criticized, even hated — that is the price."
- **Why it fails:** Em dash overuse and curly quotes are both listed AI/paste tells, and em dashes are banned vault-wide.
- **Correct:** "Being misunderstood, criticized, even hated. That is the price."
- **Why it works:** The full stop makes the last clause a sentence that has to earn itself.

**Law 8. Never invent a detail.**
If the raw thought lacks a specific, ask Ash or leave a marked blank `[like this]`. Applies to scenes, numbers, times, objects, AND sensory embellishment: a feeling or bodily state not present in the raw material ("until my head was static") is an invented detail, not colour. Flag it as inferred or cut it.
- **Violation:** "New schedules, deleted apps." (invented; the raw material never said what he tried)
- **Why it fails:** The model fills gaps with the statistically average detail, which is exactly why it reads like a thousand identical posts. Root cause of most AI tells in personal writing.
- **Correct:** "The moment I knew I was going to college I stopped working. `[what did you stop doing, and for how long?]`"
- **Why it works:** A marked blank is a working draft. A plausible invention is a lie that ships.

**Law 9. Details must be falsifiable.**
A proper noun, a number, or a time. The detail must commit to something Ash could be wrong about.
- **Violation:** "I spent months trying to fix my focus."
- **Why it fails:** Could describe anyone's Tuesday. Which month, which method? Unfalsifiable equals generic equals AI.
- **Correct:** "I asked my school bus driver to drop me a mile before my house so I could run the rest. I did that for three years, until I could afford a gym."
- **Why it works:** Bus driver, one mile, three years, a gym. Only a person who lived it can write it.

**Law 10. Short sentences must be earned.**
No two consecutive short declaratives unless each adds new information. Rhythm alone is noise.
- **Violation:** "I bunk. I mess around. I put down everything I am actually building."
- **Why it fails:** Three declaratives, near identical length, consecutive. The rhythm carries the passage and no sentence adds information. Short sentences read as confidence, which is why AI overuses them.
- **Correct:** "It was going to work. That was the problem."
- **Why it works:** The second sentence reverses the first. Each earns its stop.

**Law 11. No conclusion, no note.**
An unresolved thought stays in the catch file until Ash lands somewhere.
- **Violation:** "I still cannot tell whether that is rest or running away. Both of them feel like relief, which is the whole problem." (published as a close)
- **Why it fails:** The writer is still asking. Every note in the swipe file is someone who has finished thinking. A reader gets nothing to agree or argue with.
- **Correct:** Route the thought to `Substack Catch.md` with one line on what is unresolved.
- **Why it works:** The catch file is where thoughts finish cooking. Publishing is for verdicts.

**Law 12. Claim first.**
The note opens on the claim. Story and scene are proof and come after. (Default spine; only shape-level overrides like Quote + Endorsement are exempt.)
- **Violation:** "I caught myself reading slower so the chapter would last longer." (as line 1)
- **Why it fails:** A scene from a stranger's life earns nothing in a feed. Story-first assumes trust that has not been given. Newsletters get trust from the headline and the subscribe decision; a note has two seconds and one line.
- **Correct:** "You can procrastinate with almost anything, as long as it is respectable enough that nobody questions it." (then the reading-slower scene as proof)
- **Why it works:** The claim picks the fight; the scene proves Ash has standing to pick it. Every strong swipe note leads with the claim: "There is a sickness going around." "Most people quit because."

**Law 13. Cold open, arguable claim.**
Line 1 names its subject in plain words. No "these," "this," "that," or pronoun without a noun. Also no naked abstract nouns ("the chaos," "the work," "the noise") standing in for an unnamed subject: name the actual thing (YouTube, the metric, the plan). Test: could a stranger disagree with the sentence as written? If their honest reaction is "what do you mean," it is a mood, not a claim.
- **Self-test violation (2026-07-19):** "Some part of you needs the chaos." Same crime as "these tools," committed by this skill's own first-time output. The abstraction test was added because of it.
- **Violation:** "These tools multiply whatever you bring them."
- **Why it fails:** "These tools" points at nothing for a cold reader; four words in, they are doing decode work they never agreed to. And the sentence cannot be argued with, so it cannot be agreed with either.
- **Correct:** "AI is only good at the things you already understand."
- **Why it works:** Subject named in two characters. A stranger can say "no," which means they can also say "yes."

## PROCESS — per invocation

1. **Read the raw thought.** From chat, voice memo transcript, or `Substack Catch.md`.
2. **Law 11 gate first.** Unresolved → route to catch file, tell Ash what is missing. Stop.
3. **Run the six steps.** Strip, tension, flip, shape, anchor, cut.
4. **Draft 2 or 3 shape variants.** Default spine unless the shape overrides it.
5. **Run the thirteen-law gate on each draft.** Check every law explicitly. Fix or kill before Ash sees anything.
6. **Quality bar above the laws** (Ash's loved notes had all three; his "fine, I guess" notes were missing one):
   - The close inverts or adds a cost. A metaphor restating the opener is still a restatement.
   - A mechanism is on the page: WHY the trap works, not just that it exists.
   - A lived scene with one slightly strange detail. If the raw thought has no scene, do not ship flat: ask Ash "when did this actually bite you?" and hold the note until he answers. Only Verdict and Quote + Endorsement may ship sceneless.
   Law-compliant but flat gets redrafted once, then held rather than presented.
7. **Present.** Each draft labeled with its shape. Every inferred detail flagged: "Details I inferred, confirm or replace: ..." Marked blanks left visible.
8. **Log corrections.** Any correction Ash makes goes to Refinements, dated, verbatim.

## PROCESS — weekly session

Trigger: "notes session."
1. Mine `05 Notes and Ideas/Substack Catch.md` + the current week file (`00 Self-Management/Weeks/Week [ISO].md`) for thoughts with tension.
2. Produce 7 candidates via the per-invocation process.
3. Ash cuts to 3.
4. Clear the catch file (processed thoughts deleted, unresolved ones stay with their one-line status).
5. Accepted notes get appended to `05 Notes and Ideas/Swipe Files/Published Notes.md` (create on first accept) with date and shape, for calibration.

## CAPTURE

Ash dumps raw thoughts to `05 Notes and Ideas/Substack Catch.md` (append-only, no editing, no judging) or says "note this" in chat, which appends there. Voice memo transcripts land raw. Nothing is evaluated at capture time.
