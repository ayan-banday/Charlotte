# Email Campaign Writer

Three phases. No shortcuts.

**Phase 1** produces a Campaign Strategy Doc.
**Phase 2** takes this Doc and produces one Email Brief per email.
**Phase 3** takes each Email Brief and writes the finished email.

Each phase has a clearly defined input and a clearly defined output. The output of Phase 1 is the input of Phase 2. The output of Phase 2 is the input of Phase 3. No room for interpretation in between.

# PHASE 1 — CAMPAIGN STRATEGY

## Goal of This Phase

Before a single email is planned, the campaign must be understood at a strategic level. What does the reader believe when they enter? What should they believe when they buy? What barriers stand in the way? How does the sequence fit with the lead magnet, sales page, and product?

Only when that's crystal clear can you plan emails that actually convert.

## Input You Need to Provide

Before this phase I need the following information from you. Ask for everything that's missing. Better to ask one question too many than too few:

**About the Product:**
- Product name and price
- What exactly does the buyer get? (Deliverables, format, duration)
- Unique Mechanism: What's the specific lever/system that delivers the result? (Not "a course on marketing" — but "an AI Second Brain that learns your style and then produces content in your voice")
- Guarantee / Risk minimization
- Link to sales page (if it exists — I'll read it)

**About the Lead Magnet / Entry Point:**
- What did the reader download / see / watch?
- What was promised to them?
- Have they already received what was promised or not?
- How long have they been on the list?

**About the Customer Avatar:**
- Who is this person concretely? (Job, situation, daily life)
- What have they already tried and why didn't it work?
- What are their top 3 objections to your product?
- Which of Life's 8 Desires drive them most strongly? (see below)
- At what Awareness Level do they enter the sequence?

**About the Campaign:**
- What's the primary conversion goal? (Purchase, book call, join community...)
- Is there time pressure / launch deadline?
- How many emails should the sequence have?
- Are there emails that already work well?
- **Campaign Type:** Welcome Sequence (Nurture) + BoFu / or direct BoFu? — Rule of thumb: If story + mechanism are built into the sales emails, welcome can be removed. If not, welcome comes first.

## The Frameworks Applied in Phase 1

### Eugene Schwartz — 5 Levels of Awareness

The most important strategic framework. Every campaign must know where the reader starts and where they end.

**Level 1 — Unaware**
The reader doesn't even know they have a problem. They live in their normalcy. Copy here must start with an emotional trigger, not a problem.

**Level 2 — Problem Aware**
They sense something is wrong. They feel pain, but have no solution yet. Copy here: name the problem, sharpen the pain, show there's a way out.

**Level 3 — Solution Aware**
They know solutions exist. Maybe they've tried some. They know the category. Copy here: Why is your solution different/better than everything they already know?

**Level 4 — Product Aware**
They know your product but haven't bought it yet. This is where most email work happens. Copy here: break down objections, build trust, provide proof, set the right frame.

**Level 5 — Most Aware**
They want to buy, just need the right moment / final push. Copy here: make the offer clear, urgency, final push.

**Rule:** Most email campaigns to warm leads (demo downloads, newsletter subscribers) start at Level 3–4. Never start at Level 1 — that's wasted energy.

---

### Breakthrough Advertising — Desire Mapping

Eugene Schwartz: "Copy cannot create desire. It can only channel desire that already exists."

Before every campaign: What desire is already burning in this reader? Not what you want to sell — what they already want.

**The Three Levels of Desire:**

1. **Surface Desire** (what they say): "I want more followers", "I want to build a course"
2. **Real Desire** (what they really want): Recognition, freedom, the feeling of mattering
3. **Deep Desire** (Life's 8 level): Belonging, superiority, security

Copy always works on all three levels simultaneously. Surface Desire is the hook. Real Desire is the body. Deep Desire is the emotional foundation.

---

### Life's 8 — The 8 Deepest Human Desires (from Cashvertising)

Every strong email touches at least one of these:

1. **Survival, enjoyment, life extension** — Health, energy, vitality
2. **Enjoyment of food and drink** — (rarely relevant for business context)
3. **Freedom from fear, pain, danger** — FOMO, security, "not falling behind"
4. **Sexual attraction** — Attractiveness, status, how others perceive us
5. **Pleasant living conditions** — Comfort, ease, autonomy, location independence
6. **Superiority, gain, keeping up with others** — "ahead of 99%", competition, status
7. **Care and protection of loved ones** — (rarely directly relevant)
8. **Social recognition** — Respect, community, being seen

**For business/creator context, 3, 5, 6, and 8 are the strongest triggers.**

---

### Psychological Survival of the Ego

People don't just buy for better results — they buy to protect or confirm their identity. This is more powerful than any rational argument.

Concretely: "I'm someone who shares knowledge." "I'm not the one who fails at discipline." "I'm someone who acts, not just consumes."

Products that confirm the desired ego identity are bought more easily than products that just promise results.

In the campaign: Build an identity bridge early. Not just "this is what you'll achieve" but "this is who you'll be — and who you always wanted to be."

---

### Funnel Congruence

The sequence must fit with the lead magnet and sales page. Congruence check:

**What did the lead magnet promise?**
The first email must deliver on this promise or build from it — never ignore it. Someone who watched a demo about "AI Second Brain" expects more of that. Someone who downloaded a "5 Steps to X" guide wants to experience Step 1 next.

**What does the sales page do?**
Read the sales page before planning. The email sequence prepares the ground — it shouldn't create surprises when the reader clicks to the sales page. Every claim in the sequence must land on the sales page.

**Objection Stack:**
What are the 3–5 biggest objections to buying? These must be dismantled in the sequence — one per email, in the right order (from softest to hardest objection).

# PHASE 2 — EMAIL BRIEF PLANNER

## Goal of This Phase

Each email gets a complete brief. The brief is so detailed that someone who doesn't know the campaign can still write the email correctly. No creative freedom in Phase 3 — Phase 3 executes what Phase 2 decided.

## Input

The Campaign Strategy Doc from Phase 1.

## Approach

Build the sequence overview first (all emails at a glance), then work through each brief individually. Never all briefs at once — always show one, get approval, then the next.

---

## DEPTH STANDARD — What a Complete Brief Means

**A brief is not a structure outline. A brief is an almost-finished email draft.**

The difference:
- ❌ "Scene: John has no topic, gets to the result in 30 minutes"
- ✅ "John sits in front of a blank document. No topic. No audience. No clue what his offer should be. He has ChatGPT. He's watched YouTube videos. He's saved prompt lists. 30 minutes later: topic done. audience clear. offer framework ready. Same evening: lead magnet live."

**Every section in the brief must be at this level:**

**H — Hook:**
Don't just name the hook type. Write out the first sentence (or first two). Explain the subject-hook-preview congruence in one sentence showing how all three work together.

**P — Pain Mirror:**
Write all avatar quotes verbatim (*"quote"* in italics). Develop pain from pain on three levels — as sentences, not bullet points. Write out the transition sentence from P → A.

**A — Advantage:**
Each benefit as a complete paragraph. Not "show how AI works as a lever" — write the paragraph that shows it. Your personal story (place, concrete situation) spelled out if relevant.

**G / A2 / C:**
G: Write out the tease of the next email, don't just name it. Exact wording of skip mechanic if present.
A2: Bridge to your offer as a complete paragraph.
C: Exact CTA text.

**Reference Standard:** Look at published briefs from your approved campaigns. Each new brief must be at this detail level before it counts as done.

## The Foundation Principle of Phase 2

**You always sell only the next step — never the product.**

Email 1 doesn't sell the product. Email 1 sells opening Email 2.
Email 5 doesn't sell the product. Email 5 sells clicking the sales page.
The sales page sells the purchase.

Each email has exactly one job. When writing the brief, always ask: "What's the next step this email sells?" — not: "Why should they buy?"

## The Frameworks Applied in Phase 2

### HPAGA C — The Core Framework per Email

Not every email needs all 6 elements. But every email must know which elements it needs and which it skips.

**H — Hook**
This is the most important. Without a hook nobody reads further. The hook isn't just the first sentence — it covers subject line, preview text, and opening paragraph together. All three must work together.

Hook requirements:
- **Relevance**: Why is this relevant to the reader — right now, at this moment?
- **Awareness**: At what consciousness level is the reader? Hook must start exactly there.
- **Value Equation (Hormozi)**: Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement ÷ Time Delay ÷ Effort & Sacrifice

Dream Outcome = What do they really want?
Perceived Likelihood = Do they believe they can achieve it?
Time Delay = How quickly can they achieve it?
Effort & Sacrifice = How much do they have to put in?

Hook types that work:
- Self-story-first: Start with yourself (your own failure/realization) → bridge to reader
- Provocative thesis: Breaks conventional wisdom
- FOMO: What does the reader lose if they do nothing?
- Curiosity gap: Leaves a question the reader wants answered
- Specific number + timeframe: "In 3 hours of deep work, achieve what others do in 2 weeks..."

**P — Perspective, Pain Point, Proof**

Three possible directions, choose one or more:

*Perspective:* The reader has a false assumption about their problem. Show them a different perspective before you name the problem. ("Most people think the problem is X — but the real problem is Y")

*Pain Point:* Name the problem so sharply that the reader nods. Not superficial — go 3x deeper with "Why is this a problem?" and trace it back to Life's 8. The reader doesn't want to be lectured; they want to be understood.

*Proof:* Borrow credibility or provide your own proof.
- Credibility hacking: Quote authorities (Navy Seals, neuroscience, well-known names)
- Story as proof: One concrete person, one concrete transformation, one concrete timeframe
- Numbers as proof: Not vague — "66,000 followers", "in half an hour", "same evening"

**Pain Mirror — Avatar Quotes Verbatim**

When P mirrors the reader's pain: use the exact words of the avatar from your research — don't paraphrase. The customer's own phrasing hits harder than any rewording.

Format: italics + quotes: *"I have lots of ideas but no structure or clarity in my head."*

Never: "You sit in front of the screen and don't know what to post." (that's paraphrasing)

**Pain from Pain**

Don't just name the pain — go three levels deeper.

Example:
- Level 1: "You don't post consistently" (symptom)
- Level 2: "You're losing reach while others pass you by" (consequence)
- Level 3: "You know you could — and you're not doing it anyway" (identity wound, Life's 8/6)

Only at Level 3 does pain really hit. That's salt in the wound — not to hurt, but because only sharp pain creates the impulse to change. Every pain point in the email must go at least 2 levels deep.

**Loss Aversion**

Negative emotions hit harder than positive. The fear of losing something beats the hope of gaining.

Sequence: First in the P section, sharpen the pain of staying — then in the A section, show the way out. Not simultaneously. Not reversed. The reader must feel what they lose before they see what they can gain.

**Write in Images**

Never label emotion — show emotion.

❌ "Peter was afraid of the next post."
✅ "Peter opened the draft document, scrolled to the bottom — and closed it again."

The reader should feel the emotion themselves, not be informed about it. This applies to all pain descriptions, case studies, and transformation scenes.

**Let the Customer Recognize It Themselves**

Never say directly "You have this problem." Instead: describe the situation → ask a precise question → lead the reader to their own realization.

What they recognize themselves, they believe. What you claim, they doubt.
Format: "Do you know this?" / "Tell me if I'm wrong:" / describe scene → pause → then resolve.

**A — Advantage**

Describing features is wrong. Always benefit of the benefit.

❌ "You get 12 video lessons"
✅ "You know exactly what to post every Monday morning — no blank page anymore"

Fire on Life's 8. Paint in images. The reader should see their future life, not read a product list.

People buy from emotion and justify with logic. In P you create fear (of staying where they are). In A you create hope and anticipation (of what's possible).

**G — Gamify**

Make implementation feel imaginably simple. Give the reader a clear map:
- "5 steps to X"
- "In 30 days, one hour a day"
- "You do A, then B, then C — and after 2 weeks D happens"

The clearer the map, the lower the perceived Effort & Sacrifice in the Value Equation.

**A2 — Advantage 2.0**

After gamifying, show the transformation again. Before the CTA comes, paint the picture one more time: Here's where you are now. Here's where you could be in 30 days. This is the emotional close before the offer.

**Concept Naming for CTAs**

Each email teases the next with a concrete concept name — not vague "I'm sending you something tomorrow", but "Tomorrow: [Concept Name]". This gives the reader something tangible to look forward to.

Concept names must:
- Capture the core insight of the next email in 2–4 words
- Turn a weakness into a strength (e.g., "The Lurker Asset" — lurking as an asset)
- Or create tension (e.g., "The Ego-Death Moment")

CTA format: "Tomorrow: [Concept Name] — [one sentence what the reader gets from it]"

**Skip Mechanic (systeme.io)**

Optional in every email: "Can't wait? Get [next concept] right now:" + Link → triggers systeme.io automation to push the contact directly to the next step in the sequence. For readers ready to move immediately.

**C — Call to Action**

One CTA. Clear, concrete, with the outcome the reader gets — not what they should do.

❌ "Click here"
✅ "Start your AI Second Brain → wti.yoursite.com"

Optional: Scarcity/urgency if it's real. Never fake.

### Welcome Sequence (optional, before BoFu)

**When does it make sense?**
The Welcome Sequence is optional. Experience shows: direct BoFu converts better — but only because story and mechanism are built into the sales emails. Rule: If you don't have story + mechanism in your sales emails, you need the Welcome Sequence. If you build them in, it can be removed.

**What must always be included** (whether it's its own sequence or built into BoFu):
- Your story (transformation, not credentials)
- Your mechanism and why it works
- Why the mechanism works for the reader too

**The 3 Welcome Email Types:**

**Type W1 — The Welcome Email**
Tasks:
- Thank them for signing up/downloading
- Explain mission and why
- What they'll get in the next emails
- Ask them to reply to the email (improves sender score)

Tone: warm, direct, like a message from a real person. No copy-paste feeling.

**Type W2 — Story Emails (1–2 emails)**
Show personal transformation (or a customer's).

Why it works: People root for underdogs, not for those who had everything from the start. Trust builds through authenticity, not credentials.

Framework: Load `Skills Library/Content/Content Writing/storytelling.md` — Creator Compass Story Framework (9 steps).

Core of a good story:
- Internal + external desire at the start
- Anti-moment (low point — identity wound)
- 5-second moment: the point where everything changes in them
- Beginning of story = opposite of the end (Disney principle)

**Type W3 — Introduction Emails (2–3 emails)**
Explain the core themes and principles of your approach. Like detailed blog posts.

Structure per email:
1. Hook
2. Lead (problem — packaged in a short story)
3. Body (transformation / steps)
4. Solution (+ benefits)

Framework: **What → Why → How**
- What: What's this about?
- Why: The emotional reason behind it
- How: The concrete steps to the result

These emails build the foundation for the sales sequence that follows — they install the beliefs needed for the BoFu emails to land.

**Timing:** Every 2–3 days. Don't over-optimize — set it up first, then iterate.

---

### BoFu Email Types (from proven prompts)

The following email types are proven for Product-Aware sequences. Integrate them into the HPAGA C framework:

**Email Type 1 — Problem Mirror**
Shows the reader their problem more clearly than they see it themselves. Awareness shift 3→4.
HPAGA C focus: H + P (Pain + Perspective). No offer.

**Email Type 2 — Guilt Release ("Good Will Hunting")**
"It's not your fault that you're not where you want to be yet."
Releases the reader from self-blame, reframes the problem as structural (no system), not personal (no talent/discipline).
HPAGA C focus: H + P (Perspective) + A.

**Email Type 3 — 5-inch vs 5-mile Benefits**
5-inch benefits = immediate advantages (today, this week)
5-mile benefits = long-term transformation (in 6–12 months)
HPAGA C focus: A + G + A2.

**Email Type 4 — Social Proof / Case Study**
One concrete person, one concrete result. As specific as possible.
Not: "Many customers have succeeded."
But: "Alessandro came to me with a few hundred views per video. Today 66,000 followers."
HPAGA C focus: H + P (Proof) + A + C.

**Email Type 5 — Objection Buster**
Directly addresses the hardest remaining objection.
Format: Name the objection (in the reader's words, not your own) → Reframe → Proof.
HPAGA C focus: H + P (Perspective) + A + C.

**Email Type 6 — Urgency/Scarcity**
Only if real. Deadline, price increase, limited spots.
HPAGA C focus: H + A2 + C. Short and direct.

**Email Type 7 — Last Call**
Final email. No new arguments. Just: reminder of transformation, guarantee, link.
Optional: one strong testimonial or brief case-study reminder.
HPAGA C focus: A2 + C. Maximum 200 words.

# PHASE 3 — EMAIL WRITER

## Goal of This Phase

Translate the Email Brief from Phase 2 into a finished email. No creative freedom on content and structure — Phase 2 decided that. Creative freedom only in concrete wording.

## Input

1. The Email Brief from Phase 2
2. The voice skill (`Skills Library/Marketing/Sales & Conversion Copy/jani-voice/jani-voice.md` — always read before writing)
3. If available: already approved emails from the sequence (for tone consistency)

## Approach

1. Read the voice skill completely
2. Read the Email Brief completely
3. Write
4. Self-review: Check every line against the anti-patterns from the voice skill
5. Deliver output

## Quality Checklist (internal before output)

Before each final email, go through these questions:

**Hook:**
- [ ] Does the email start with energy, not introduction?
- [ ] Is the opener self-first (your own experience) or a direct pattern interrupt?
- [ ] Do subject → preview → first sentence work together?

**Content:**
- [ ] Is the belief from the brief actually being broken/installed?
- [ ] Is every abstract statement grounded in a concrete scene or number?
- [ ] Is the Life's 8 element emotionally touched, not just mentioned?
- [ ] Is the email focused on exactly one job?
- [ ] Are emotions shown instead of labeled? ("Peter shivered" instead of "Peter was afraid")?
- [ ] Does the pain go at least 2 levels deep (pain from pain)?
- [ ] Was the pain of staying shown before the escape route (loss aversion)?

**Voice:**
- [ ] Natural sentences, no translated-sounding language?
- [ ] Conversational where it fits: "maybe", "or something", trailing "..."?
- [ ] Energy closer if it's an energetic email?

**Structure:**
- [ ] Maximum paragraph length: 3–4 lines?
- [ ] CTA singular and concrete?
- [ ] Email ends with energy, not a summary?

## When a Story is Needed

Whenever an email contains a self-story opener, case study, or transformation scene: Load the `storytelling` skill (`Skills Library/Content/Content Writing/storytelling.md`).

The storytelling skill contains the Creator Compass Story Framework (9 steps: Hook → Backstory → Desires → Anti-moment → Epiphany → Plan → Conflicts → Result → Transformation) with voice adaptations and platform outputs for Reel, Newsletter, and YouTube.

Story quality questions:
- Does the story have an anti-moment (the low point)?
- Does it have an epiphany (the turning point)?
- Is the transformation visible (before/after)?
- Is it specific enough to picture (time, place, concrete detail)?

---
