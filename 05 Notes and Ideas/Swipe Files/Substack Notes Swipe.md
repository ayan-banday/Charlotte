**01**
There is a sickness going around where we believe the ultimate goal is a million dollars and a personal brand. It's a trap. The actual dream? Just enough money to pay the bills, a craft you love, 1,000 readers who truly care, and shutting your laptop at 5:00 PM to go eat dinner with your family. We don't need empires. We need our lives back.

**02**
AI brings ghostwriting to the masses and you can expect the works to be equally good.

**03**
A vision only gets clearer the more you try move toward it.

Pick a goal. Any goal. Try achieve it. Fail as much as you can. Reflect on why you failed, and learn to iterate ruthlessly.

That is how you understand, as fast as is possible, what life you do not want, which, funnily enough, will always lead you closer to a life you do end up wanting.

**04**
learn to disappoint people or they'll design your life for you

**05**
your worst sin is that you have destroyed and betrayed yourself for a job.

**06**
One of the best antidepressants I've found is astrophysics. It's hard to take my life too seriously when there's a black hole at the centre of the Milky Way 4 million times the mass of our sun, or seeing light from stars that happened over 10,000 years ago in the present moment.

**07**
we're living through what Joseph Campbell would call the "belly of the whale." the old myths don't hold anymore. careers, institutions, religions, all hollowing out. the new myths haven't arrived yet. so everyone walks around in this mythological gap feeling vaguely lost and calling it anxiety. the feeling is civilizational. the discomfort of living in a between-story. and it's accurate.

**08**
Most social media makes you want to keep scrolling.

After a few minutes on Substack, all you want to do is get off, make some tea, read a book, and write something lovely.

**09**
I think this is spot on:

"If you're going to use an LLM to write me an email, I'd much rather you just send me the prompt; at least then I'd have an idea of what you actually meant to say."

**10**
If you don't have a goal so meaningful it makes other people's opinions irrelevant, you will lose control of your life. You will adopt the goals assigned to you by your parents, peers, or society.

**11**
The 2-4 hours you spend scrolling each day (or 730-1460 hours each year) is more than enough time to write a book, build a business, or get in shape. In the moment, it seems like nothing. That's why it's so dangerous. Your time disappears without you being conscious of it.

**12**
How to master any skill fast:

- stop studying

- outline a project

- start building it

- hit a roadblock

- figure out how to overcome it

- repeat 4 and 5 for the rest of your life

Most people don't get past 1, the rest spiral into complacency after 4.

**13**
The human mind did not evolve over thousands of years to have hundreds of unfiltered opinions thrown at it every hour through a screen. You wouldn't let 100 random people break into your house, eat your food, and sleep there. Yet you willingly do that with your mind.

**14**
The advice for zero to one internet entrepreneurship to "just build something" has lost such an enormous amount of value in a short period of time with the explosion of LLMs, I propose a new idiom: "just risk something."

This has always been true, but you used to have to risk a significant amount more time to get an idea out of your head and onto the internet.

It is also very hard to get out of the skeuomorphic paradigm of creating old things very quickly and feeling accomplished, and good to remind yourself constantly that risk/discomfort is a better proxy for wether or not anyone else will attempt what you're doing, and to what extent.

**15**
Most people quit because they forget that you have to be bad at something before you can be good at it. It's so obvious. You suck. Of course you're not going to win in 2 weeks. But if you can learn to enjoy extended periods of failure, you will make it very, very far in life.

**16**
It's a simple fact of life: if you want to do something incredible, something that makes you stand out above the rest, then you have to become comfortable being different, that means being misunderstood, criticized, even hated.

**17**
It took me too many years to learn this, but you have to develop the ability to be disliked in order to free yourself from the prison of other people's opinions.

**18**
If you want to achieve anything great, it needs to become your one true priority. The only thing on your mind. Nobody accidentally got rich from business. Nobody accidentally built a great physique. They were obsessed with it for multiple years until it became their default.

**19**
A pattern I've noticed in stuck people:

They're always busy. They never stop moving. They have 47 tabs open and a notebook-sized to-do list. But if you ask them what they accomplished this week that actually matters, their mind goes blank.

Busyness isn't a badge of honor.

**20**
"I'm not a creative person."

No, you are, everyone is, but your mind is just clogged by all of the podcasts and social media you ram into it without properly digesting it. You're conditioned to believe you can only take a certain path in life, and you don't daydream or entertain stupid ideas that could set you on an entirely new trajectory. You're so obsessed with being productive and efficient that you feel like you're always falling behind, and that stress prevents you from thinking outside the box.

You need to slow the fuck down, allow yourself to be bored (actually bored, not so overstimulated that you find enjoyable things boring), and pursue a life that you design, not one that was assigned to you.

**21**
Your brain works against you until you give it a meaningful goal to wire itself around.
