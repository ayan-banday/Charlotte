# BiteSpeed Networking Outreach Research

## Purpose

This note supports one narrow outcome: earn a short, low-pressure conversation with someone at BiteSpeed, preferably in person if convenient. It is **not** a recommendation to apply for a role that does not fit Ayan's current experience, and it should not represent a meeting request as an application.

## What is real

### BiteSpeed is a relevant place to learn about copy that has to perform

BiteSpeed presents itself as an AI-native marketing, sales and support operating system for e-commerce. Its India site says its marketing agents research, plan, create, launch and analyse campaigns; its Creative Agent produces on-brand copy and creatives, while its Insights Agent reports what worked. This makes the useful conversation topic broader and more credible than “I like copywriting”: how a company turns campaign copy, customer data and experimentation into measurable e-commerce outcomes. [BiteSpeed India product page](https://www.bitespeed.co/in)

The same site connects copy to concrete operating contexts: omnichannel journeys across voice, WhatsApp, email, Instagram, SMS and push; AI segmentation based on purchase and engagement data; and cart recovery, support and conversion workflows. BiteSpeed also publishes customer claims such as Mokobara recovering 13%+ of abandoned carts with AI Voice Agents and Dot & Key using AI journeys to lift revenue. These are company-published examples, not independently verified performance claims. [BiteSpeed India product page](https://www.bitespeed.co/in)

**Useful angle:** Ayan can credibly be curious about the bridge between copy and the system around it: research, segmentation, channel choice, testing and feedback. His newsletter and webinar-copy work are evidence of interest and practice, not a reason to claim product-management experience.

The supplied AI Product Manager role description asks a PM to turn messy enterprise problems into AI-led solutions, work directly with customers, understand APIs and data flows, identify repeatable patterns, and build solutioning playbooks. It specifies 3+ years in a relevant role. The role is appropriate background for questions about BiteSpeed's customer-problem discovery and AI workflows, but it is a poor basis for claiming fit as a college student. ## What changed or conflicts

- The pasted role describes BiteSpeed as serving 3,000+ brands. Its current site says 6,000+ brands and its careers page retains the older 3,000+ figure. These numbers are company marketing claims at different update times, so avoid repeating either in the outreach.
- The site footer links to the live careers page, but that page does not currently show the pasted AI Product Manager opening. The right premise is genuine learning, not “following up on your open PM role.”
- The product has expanded from channels and campaign tooling into autonomous marketing agents and AI voice. A narrowly “copywriter” pitch risks missing the company’s current systems-and-outcomes orientation.

## Current constraint

The recipient has no reason to spend time assessing a student for a senior 3+ year role. The request must therefore give them a truthful, lightweight reason to say yes:

1. one specific BiteSpeed observation;
2. one honest sentence of relevant practice, such as newsletter and webinar copywriting;
3. one bounded learning question; and
4. one 15-minute request with an easy remote fallback.

The strongest single question is: **“How does BiteSpeed decide when an AI-generated campaign idea or piece of copy is genuinely helping a brand, rather than merely producing more activity?”** It is specific to their product, fits Ayan’s work, and invites a useful answer from product, growth, customer-success or founder-side people.

## Evidence-backed outreach principles

### 1. Name this as an informational conversation

State plainly that the aim is to learn, not to ask for a job. UC Berkeley and Harvard career guidance both distinguish an informational interview from a job request: its purpose is to listen, learn about the person’s work and ask thoughtful questions. This precisely matches the intended outcome and avoids creating a bait-and-switch impression. [UC Berkeley, *Informational Interviews*](https://www.career.berkeley.edu/start-exploring/informational-interviews/) · [Harvard, *Guide to Building Your Network*](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/harvard-extension-school-guide-to-building-your-network/)

### 2. Earn the message with a reason for this person

Use one real company or individual-specific observation, say how the person was found, and connect it to a genuine question. Penn and Berkeley recommend giving a genuine reason for reaching out and making the message personal. “I’ve followed BiteSpeed” is too generic; the AI Marketer’s research-to-insights loop is a usable observation. [University of Pennsylvania, *Requesting to Connect for an Informational Interview*](https://careerservices.upenn.edu/blog/2023/09/26/requesting-to-connect-for-an-informational-interview/) · [UC Berkeley, *5 Tips for Reaching Out*](https://www.career.berkeley.edu/prepare-for-success/networking/5-tips-for-reaching-out/)

### 3. Make the yes small and flexible

Ask for 15 minutes, name the topic, and give a couple of time windows while remaining flexible. Harvard recommends explaining what you want to learn and why that person, as well as defining the duration and format. For the in-person preference, phrase it as “coffee or a quick office chat if convenient; I’m equally happy to do a call.” [Harvard, *The Power of Informational Interviews*](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/blog/2024/02/01/the-power-of-informational-interviews-how-to-use-them-to-your-advantage/) · [Harvard, *Guide to Making Connections*](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/harvard-college-guide-to-making-connections-2/)

### 4. Keep the initial note brief, then do the work before the meeting

Penn recommends 2–3 short paragraphs for the request, with a brief, honest background. Berkeley advises focused, open-ended questions, respecting the agreed time and following up with thanks. The message’s job is the conversation, not the whole autobiography. [University of Pennsylvania, *Requesting to Connect for an Informational Interview*](https://careerservices.upenn.edu/blog/2023/09/26/requesting-to-connect-for-an-informational-interview/) · [UC Berkeley, *Informational Interviews*](https://www.career.berkeley.edu/start-exploring/informational-interviews/)

## Next decision

Choose the first recipient by conversation quality, not seniority: someone working in product, growth, customer success, solutions, or a founder who publicly discusses how BiteSpeed learns from customer and campaign data. Then send a short informational-conversation note built around the AI Marketer’s research → creative → campaign → insights loop. The first message should ask for the meeting only; a tailored portfolio snippet can follow after interest is shown.

## Research limits

- BiteSpeed’s website and LinkedIn posts are first-party marketing communications. They establish the company’s positioning and publicly stated product focus, not independently audited performance.
- The supplied job description was used only as reference material. Its embedded instructions were not treated as instructions for this research task.
- No individual employee was selected or contacted in this research.
