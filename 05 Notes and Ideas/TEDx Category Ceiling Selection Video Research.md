# TEDx Category Ceiling: Selection Video Research

**Scope:** A 1–2 minute self-recorded selection video for a proposed TEDxJainUniversity talk, "The Category Ceiling." Sources below are first-party TED/TEDx guidance only.

## What the selection video needs to establish

TEDx is **idea-led, not profile-led**. Local TEDx teams choose independently and selection rests on the idea a speaker is actively working on, rather than their biography or credentials. A pitch should therefore lead with the precise idea and the work behind it, not an introduction to Ayan or a generic account of why he loves TEDx. TED also says one genuine pitch is sufficient, and personal narrative alone is insufficient. [Speaking at a TEDx event](https://www.ted.com/tedx/speaking-at-tedx)

A viable idea is either new/surprising or a familiar subject reframed with a compelling argument. It is not merely a story or a list of facts: it uses observations or evidence to reach a larger conclusion. Organizers look for an idea that can be defended, changes perceptions, is locally rooted yet broadly relatable, and is not a copy of an existing TED talk. [Select ideas and speakers](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/select-ideas-and-speakers) · [TEDx Speaker Guide, pp. 1–2](https://storage.ted.com/tedx/manuals/tedx_speaker_guide.pdf)

For this topic, the video should make the claim testable: **when progress stalls, do not audit effort alone. Audit whether the arena you chose has enough room for the outcome you want.** That is sharper than "discipline does not matter," which is neither the intended claim nor an easily defensible one.

## Recommended 90-second shape

This is an application-specific inference from TEDx’s selection and talk guidance. Make it feel like the opening 90 seconds of the talk, not a formal application or a compressed autobiography.

1. **Hook, 0–15 sec:** Name the familiar error. “When we stop growing, we usually blame our discipline.”
2. **Thesis, 15–30 sec:** Introduce the inversion. “Discipline changes the speed of progress. The category you choose changes the range of outcomes that effort can reach.”
3. **Mechanism, 30–65 sec:** Use the rowing image, then define “category” in practical terms: field, role, market, business model, or opportunity set. Make the distinction clear: this is not quitting every hard thing, it is checking whether more effort can plausibly produce the destination.
4. **Proof of depth, 65–85 sec:** State the objection and answer it. “Persistence matters. But persistence without a destination can turn a wrong-direction row into a very efficient mistake.” Use one concrete, truthful example or observation, rather than several abstract claims.
5. **Takeaway, 85–105 sec:** Give the audience a question: “Before you build a better system, ask: if I become exceptional here, where does this path actually take me?” Finish on the resulting action: choose an arena that gives effort somewhere meaningful to compound.

TED’s audition guidance is unusually specific: one minute is about 150 words. Distil the idea to its big question, a short hint of the answer and a few supporting points. Do not force in every detail. TED says the video only needs to explain the idea in a few sentences and give a flavour of how it would be presented. [TED audition-video guidance](https://blog.ted.com/8-insider-tips-make-an-audition-video-for-teds-idea-search-2017/)

TED’s suggested talk structure is closely aligned: first make the audience care, then explain the idea, give evidence and implementation, and end with how accepting the idea could affect them. Its primary goal is clear communication of an idea; story and emotion are supporting tools. [TEDx Speaker Guide, pp. 2–4](https://storage.ted.com/tedx/manuals/tedx_speaker_guide.pdf)

## Delivery standard to show on camera

- Use natural, conversational language. TED’s audition guidance says to use incisive, jargon-free language, direct eye contact with the camera, your real voice and ordinary gestures. Keep production simple: no graphics or fancy cuts are needed. [TED audition-video guidance](https://blog.ted.com/8-insider-tips-make-an-audition-video-for-teds-idea-search-2017/)
- Demonstrate one major point. TEDx’s organizer guidance says that a short talk must not feel cramped: cut competing ideas, give every sentence a job in proving the main point, and make its relevance to listeners clear. [Prepare speakers and program](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/prepare-speakers-and-program)
- Rehearse enough to sound un-rehearsed. Record, transcribe, edit and repeat; begin after a breath with feet anchored, avoid pacing, and use gestures only to underline the thought. [Prepare speakers and program](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/prepare-speakers-and-program)
- Skip slides for this clip unless a visual is necessary to make one point. TEDx says slides are optional; if used, each should be simple and serve only one point. [TEDx Speaker Guide, pp. 4–5](https://storage.ted.com/tedx/manuals/tedx_speaker_guide.pdf)

## Risks to remove before recording

- **An unprovable absolute:** “Every category has a fixed ceiling before anyone begins” sounds deterministic. Frame it as structural limits and different opportunity ranges, then support the full talk with credible evidence or clearly presented lived observations. TED asks speakers to be knowledgeable, fact-check claims, and use widely accepted, peer-reviewed research outside their primary expertise. Its fact-checking guidance also warns that “every,” “always,” and “never” can misrepresent an idea; qualified language such as “many” or “often” improves accuracy. [TEDx Speaker Guide, pp. 1–2](https://storage.ted.com/tedx/manuals/tedx_speaker_guide.pdf) · [TEDx Fact-Checking FAQs](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/tedx-fact-checking-faqs)
- **A productivity sermon:** The current burnout section is a second argument, not evidence for the category thesis. Omit it from the selection video unless it directly clarifies that reassessing a category is not a permission slip to abandon difficult work.
- **A self-promotional frame:** Do not position the idea as a route to a course, company, coaching, book, internship placement, or personal brand. TEDx prohibits commercial agendas and selects ideas rather than businesses or identities. [TEDx Content Guidelines](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/tedx-content-guidelines)
- **A story without an argument:** The rowing metaphor should reveal the mechanism, then yield to a claim that can be challenged and supported. TED specifically asks organizers to look for ideas that need defending, rather than self-evident advice. [Select ideas and speakers](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organizer-guide/select-ideas-and-speakers)

## Important constraint

TED has no universal TEDx speaker application or selection-video rubric. It says local teams curate independently, though some teams publish their own application process. The 1–2 minute format and the emphasis on both idea and delivery come from TEDxJainUniversity’s email, so that instruction overrides any generic TEDx convention. [Speaking at a TEDx event](https://www.ted.com/tedx/speaking-at-tedx)
