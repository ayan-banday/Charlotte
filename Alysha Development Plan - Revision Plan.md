# Alysha Development Plan: Revision Plan

## Purpose

This document outlines the revisions required for the Alysha developmental plan. It is intended to guide the next version of the project document and keep the scope, deliverables, and business model realistic.

## 1. Project identity

- Use **Alysha** as the project name throughout.
- Remove any incorrect project names or labels.
- Refer to the advisor as **Shradha mam**.
- Replace coded labels such as D1, D2, B1, and B2 with clear section names.

## 2. Updated project direction

Alysha is a packaging concept intended to improve the shelf life and handling of G9 bananas while providing a trust feature for identifying organic or chemical-free produce.

The project should focus on developing and validating the packaging concept. It should not present the final material, performance, or commercial outcome as already confirmed.

## 3. Problem statement revisions

The problem statement should explain that G9 bananas can lose value because of premature ripening, bruising, compression, and damage during handling, transport, and storage.

It should identify the agri-business, distributor, or produce buyer as the likely paying customer because that party manages the produce after purchase and bears the commercial impact of damaged or unsellable stock.

Farmers and end consumers should be presented as important stakeholders and beneficiaries. The organic or chemical-free trust feature should be stated as a product requirement, while avoiding any claim that packaging alone proves certification.

The material should be described as a bio-plastic solution under research. The packaging is designed for G9 bananas; the source of the material does not need to be made a major part of the explanation.

## 4. Product requirements

The revised plan should include five core requirements with a clear status for each requirement:

1. Extend the acceptable shelf life of G9 bananas.
2. Reduce bruising and handling damage.
3. Investigate resistance to compression during stacking and transport.
4. Use a suitable bio-plastic material selected through research.
5. Include a trust feature for organic or chemical-free produce.

Compression protection should remain an open development objective. The project may establish that the packaging improves protection, or it may identify limitations that require a later design iteration. It should not promise complete protection before testing.

## 5. Bio-plastic research

Before finalizing the material, compare relevant bio-plastic options using criteria such as:

- Food-contact safety
- Strength and flexibility
- Moisture resistance
- Suitability for banana packaging
- Effect on shelf life
- Cost and availability
- End-of-life characteristics

The selected material and the reasons for selecting it should be recorded in the final materials brief.

## 6. Business model

The initial business model should remain simple and linked to deliverables:

1. Develop and validate the packaging concept.
2. Produce a usable prototype or small completed batch.
3. Sell the completed packages wholesale to agri-businesses, distributors, or produce sellers.
4. Explore additional income streams only after the initial product and milestones have been validated.

The plan should not assume that OEDC or another partner will pay before the product is completed. It should also avoid presenting unconfirmed funding figures or student cohorts as part of the business model.

## 7. Milestones and deliverables

Each milestone should produce a concrete output:

| Milestone | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| Problem confirmation | Locked problem statement with the intended buyer identified |
| Requirement definition | Five solution requirements marked as confirmed, stretch, or requiring validation |
| G9 research | Short research brief covering banana handling, ripening, and shelf-life factors |
| Material research | Comparative bio-plastic research and a preliminary material recommendation |
| Initial design | Packaging concept, dimensions, construction approach, and trust-feature concept |
| Prototype development | Physical prototype or small prototype batch |
| Validation | Testing notes covering shelf life, bruising, stacking, compression, handling, and usability |
| Commercial review | Preliminary cost, wholesale pricing logic, and buyer feedback |
| Final handover | Final prototype, development record, budget and reimbursement record, and recommended next build step |

## 8. Final deliverable

The main final deliverable should be a usable prototype of the Alysha bio-plastic package, supported by:

- The locked problem statement
- The solution requirements and their validation status
- The G9 banana research brief
- The bio-plastic materials brief
- The testing and validation record
- The budget and reimbursement record
- A basic wholesale business model
- A recommended next development step

A patent or other intellectual-property assessment may be included only if the prototype contains a sufficiently distinctive and potentially protectable feature. It should be treated as an optional follow-up, not as a guaranteed deliverable.

## 9. Timeline

The existing timeline structure can be retained. It should present development estimates rather than guaranteed commercial deadlines:

- **Best case:** approximately 10 days
- **Realistic case:** approximately 20 days
- **Worst case:** up to 2 months

The timeline should account for material research, prototype development, testing, iteration, and final documentation.

## 10. Final editing checklist

Before the developmental plan is finalized:

- Use **Alysha** consistently.
- Use **Shradha mam** consistently.
- Remove coded section labels and internal references.
- Keep the explanation of the G9 banana application direct and brief.
- Distinguish confirmed facts, project requirements, assumptions, and research questions.
- Avoid unsupported claims about shelf-life extension, waste reduction, compression protection, or organic status.
- Ensure the business model, milestones, and final deliverables agree with one another.
- Keep the writing suitable for a reader who has not seen the earlier drafts or feedback.
