# Udyaan
## G9 Banana Packaging Development Project

## Project Documentation

### Project purpose


This Udyaan project is focused on improving the shelf life and packaging suitability of G9 bananas after harvest. It will investigate a suitable bio-plastic material, develop a packaging prototype, test its performance, and assess whether it can become a viable wholesale product for agri-businesses.

---

## 1. Problem Statement

G9 bananas begin to lose value after harvest because they continue to ripen and their quality changes during transport and storage. When the fruit reaches the buyer in poor condition, part of the shipment may need to be discounted, rejected, or sold before it reaches its intended market.

The problem affects several groups. Agri-businesses and produce buyers face the direct commercial impact because they purchase, handle, transport, and sell the bananas. Farmers are affected when produce is rejected or receives a lower value. Consumers are affected when bananas have a shorter usable life or when there is limited information available about whether produce is organic or chemical-free.

Current packaging is generally treated as a container rather than as a system designed around the specific needs of the fruit. It may not adequately manage the conditions that accelerate ripening, and may not provide a clear trust feature for organic or chemical-free produce. Conventional packaging can also create sustainability concerns depending on the material used and its end-of-life treatment.

The problem persists because cold storage and refrigerated transport are not always practical or affordable for every movement of fresh produce. Packaging therefore has to perform effectively under ordinary storage and transport conditions. A solution also needs to be affordable and practical for the businesses that would purchase it.

The project will investigate whether a bio-plastic packaging design can help maintain the quality of G9 bananas for longer and communicate a credible organic or chemical-free trust feature. If successful, the solution could reduce avoidable product loss, improve the value of shipments, support better buyer confidence, and create a wholesale packaging opportunity for agri-businesses.

### Primary customer and stakeholders

| Group | Relationship to the project |
|---|---|
| Agri-business or produce buyer, such as Oye Desi | Primary potential customer and wholesale buyer |
| Farmers | Suppliers who may benefit from fewer rejected or downgraded bananas |
| Transporters and handlers | Users who load, stack, move, and unload the packages |
| Retailers and final buyers | Users who need bananas to remain presentable and usable |
| Consumers | Beneficiaries of better quality and clearer product information |
| Project team and academic advisors | Developers, reviewers, and validators of the prototype |

## 2. Work Breakdown & Milestones

The project is divided into milestones. Each milestone has a defined output and an exit criterion so that progress can be reviewed objectively.

### Milestone 1: Problem and user confirmation

**Tasks**

- Confirm the G9 banana use case.
- Confirm the main buyer and user groups.
- Document the transport, storage, and shelf-life problems.
- Confirm the required organic or chemical-free trust feature.

**Output:** Locked problem statement and stakeholder summary.

**Exit criterion:** A reader outside the team can identify the affected groups, the current problem, the reason it persists, and the intended change.

### Milestone 2: Requirements definition

**Tasks**

- Define the required packaging functions.
- Separate confirmed requirements from features requiring validation.
- Define the basic acceptance tests for shelf life, package usability, and trust communication.

**Output:** Requirements sheet with status for each requirement.

**Exit criterion:** The team has an agreed list of requirements and knows how each one will be reviewed.

### Milestone 3: G9 banana and packaging research

**Tasks**

- Review the storage needs of G9 bananas.
- Study factors related to ripening, ventilation, moisture, and temperature.
- Review relevant packaging formats and their limitations.

**Output:** G9 research brief and packaging reference summary.

**Exit criterion:** The design decisions are supported by a documented understanding of the banana and its storage and transport conditions.

### Milestone 4: Bio-plastic material research

**Tasks**

- Identify suitable bio-plastic material options.
- Compare food-contact safety, strength, flexibility, moisture resistance, availability, cost, and end-of-life characteristics.
- Consider whether each material is suitable for the planned packaging structure and trust feature.

**Output:** Materials comparison and preliminary material recommendation.

**Exit criterion:** The team has selected a material direction for prototyping or documented why further research is required.

### Milestone 5: Packaging concept development

**Tasks**

- Develop the package structure and dimensions.
- Plan ventilation, access, storage, and usability features.
- Define how the package will communicate the organic or chemical-free trust feature.
**Output:** Packaging concept sheet with sketches, dimensions, material direction, and trust-feature concept.

**Exit criterion:** The concept is sufficiently defined to build a prototype.

### Milestone 6: Prototype development

**Tasks**

- Build the first physical prototype or small prototype batch.
- Record material quantities, construction method, and estimated production cost.
- Check whether the package can be loaded, stacked, moved, opened, and displayed practically.

**Output:** Physical prototype and prototype build record.

**Exit criterion:** A usable prototype exists and can be tested under defined conditions.

### Milestone 7: Testing and validation

**Tasks**

- Compare packaged bananas with the current or ordinary packaging approach where possible.
- Observe shelf-life indicators over the test period.
- Record shelf-life, temperature, moisture, and usability observations.
- Review the trust feature for clarity and credibility.
- Document limitations and required design changes.

**Output:** Validation memo with observations, photographs or records where available, and recommended changes.

**Exit criterion:** The team can state which requirements the prototype meets, which require iteration, and which remain unverified.

### Milestone 8: Commercial and handover review

**Tasks**

- Estimate material and production cost.
- Define an initial wholesale pricing approach.
- Gather feedback from the intended business user where available.
- Record the budget and reimbursements.
- Recommend the next build, pilot, or research step.

**Output:** Commercial review and final handover package.

**Exit criterion:** The project has a completed prototype, supporting records, a basic business model, and a clearly stated next step.

## 3. Revenue Opportunities

The initial model is deliberately simple: develop a usable package and sell completed packages wholesale to businesses that handle fresh produce. Additional revenue streams can be considered after the first product and validation milestones are complete.

**Still, more in-depth research is needed.**

| Opportunity | Offering | Paying customer | Monetisation model | Required proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wholesale packaging sales | Completed packages supplied in batches | Agri-businesses, distributors, and produce sellers | Product sale | Prototype, cost estimate, and buyer feedback |
| Custom packaging development | Adaptation of the package for a buyer's supply-chain needs | Agri-business or produce brand | Service fee plus development cost | Validated base design and documented requirements |
| Packaging supply agreement | Recurring supply of packages after successful adoption | Regular wholesale buyer | Contracted product sale or subscription-style supply agreement | Repeat-use evidence and reliable production process |
| Design or process licensing | Permission to use a validated packaging design or process | Packaging manufacturer or produce company | Licensing fee or royalty | Distinctive design, documented process, and legal review |
| Trust-feature integration | Packaging information or traceability feature integrated into supply | Produce brand or agri-business | Service fee, product margin, or commission depending on the system | Working feature and credible verification process |

The first commercial focus should remain wholesale packaging sales. Service, licensing, recurring supply, and commission-based opportunities should only be pursued when the underlying product and process have been validated.

## 6. Social Impact

The project is intended to reduce avoidable loss and improve confidence in fresh produce packaging. Its impact should be measured through the prototype and pilot evidence rather than through unsupported estimates.

### Intended effects

- Longer period in which bananas remain acceptable for sale or consumption.
- Better visibility of organic or chemical-free product information.
- Reduced dependence on packaging that is unsuitable for the intended use or difficult to manage at end of life.
- Better value retention for agri-businesses and fewer rejected or downgraded bananas for farmers.

### Measurement framework

| Impact area | Baseline | Proposed measure |
|---|---|---|
| Shelf life | Bananas stored using the current or ordinary packaging method | Days until the product reaches the agreed unacceptable-quality threshold |
| Buyer confidence | Existing packaging information | User feedback on clarity and credibility of the organic or chemical-free trust feature |
| Material suitability | Current packaging material and disposal practice | Material safety, performance, cost, availability, and end-of-life comparison |

The baseline should be recorded before final testing. If a controlled comparison is not possible, the document should identify the result as an observation rather than a confirmed impact measurement.

## 7. Final Deliverable

### Primary deliverable

A usable physical prototype, or small prototype batch, of the bio-plastic packaging for G9 bananas.

### Supporting handover package

The prototype will be handed over with:

1. Locked problem statement and stakeholder summary.
2. Requirements sheet covering food safety, temperature effects, bio-plastic suitability, moisture resistance, shelf-life impact, cost, manufacturability, end-of-life or combustion effects, and other relevant packaging factors, with each requirement marked as confirmed, requiring further research, or requiring validation.
3. G9 banana and packaging research brief.
4. Bio-plastic materials comparison and recommendation.
5. Packaging concept sheet with dimensions and trust-feature design.
6. Prototype build record and estimated unit cost.
7. Testing and validation memo.
8. Budget and reimbursement record.
9. Basic wholesale business model.
10. Recommended next build, pilot, or research step.

### Recipient

The final package will be presented to the project authorities, advisors, and intended business stakeholders, including relevant Oye Desi representatives where applicable.

### Acceptance criteria

The final deliverable will be considered complete when:

- A physical prototype or small prototype batch has been produced.
- The package is designed specifically for G9 bananas.
- The selected or proposed bio-plastic material is documented with supporting research.
- The organic or chemical-free trust feature is included as a defined part of the package.
- Shelf-life, material, and package performance have been tested or clearly marked as requiring further research or validation.
- The prototype's limitations are documented honestly.
- A preliminary cost and wholesale sales model are included.
- The work is supported by a clear validation record and recommended next step.

A patent or other intellectual-property filing is not a required final deliverable. It may be considered later if the prototype includes a distinctive, potentially protectable feature and an appropriate review confirms that further action is worthwhile.
