# Finance Learning Plan

## What I am trying to do

I want to build the financial discipline to use limited money, time, and team capacity wisely while building scalable businesses enabled by technology.

The goal is to understand how a business creates value, uses resources, grows, and becomes valuable over time. I want to learn these things before I have to make decisions with large amounts of money.

## How I will learn

I will begin with a large business question, then learn the smaller ideas needed to answer it. I will apply the answer to a live business situation or realistic case, present what I have learned, use feedback to find the gaps, and improve the next version.

The questions below are grouped into the three areas I am working on.

## 1. Understand whether a business can create and keep value

This area is about seeing the financial reality of a business. It begins with the problem, moves through the cost of solving it, and ends with a long-term picture of whether the business can survive and grow.

### Is the problem worth solving?

- How do I know a problem is worth solving?
- What outcome would be valuable enough for a customer to care about?
- How can I estimate the size of the problem and the value of solving it?
- How do I model what it will cost to solve the problem?
- What needs to be true for the solution to work?
- What is the smallest test that can show whether the solution is likely to work?

### How do I decide what to charge?

- What financial value does the solution create for the customer?
- Why is that outcome worth paying for?
- What does it cost to win, serve, and retain one customer?
- How much should the business charge so that it can serve customers well and still have money to grow?
- How does changing the price affect demand, the money available, and future choices?

### How do I understand what is happening financially?

- What is the difference between money earned through sales, money left after costs, and cash available to use?
- What does the business own, what does it owe, and what commitments are coming next?
- How do I identify costs that grow as the business grows?
- How do I tell whether the business is becoming stronger, weaker, or simply busier?
- How do I recognise early signs that the business is running short of money?

### How do I build a five-year financial plan?

- How much money could the business earn, spend, and keep each year?
- How much time and team capacity would be needed at each stage?
- How much money must remain available while the business grows?
- What changes if the business grows faster, grows slowly, or faces a difficult year?
- Which assumptions make the biggest difference to the plan?

## 2. Decide where limited money, time, and people should go

This area is about making good spending decisions. The same question applies whether I am deciding about research, a team member, a tool, marketing, or my own time.

### How do I decide where the next rupee and hour should go?

- Which use of money or time could create the most value for the business?
- What should come first: building the product, finding customers, improving delivery, or strengthening the team?
- What will this choice prevent me from doing?
- How much can I spend now while keeping enough money for the business to continue?
- How do I compare an early return with a bigger result that may take longer?

### How do I decide what a team member, tool, or research project is worth paying for?

- How much should I pay a team member, and what should determine that amount?
- What is the full cost of bringing a person into the business?
- When should I hire, work with someone temporarily, use a tool, build the capability internally, or wait?
- How much should I spend on research before the evidence says continue, change direction, or stop?
- What useful result must a tool, person, or research project produce to justify its cost?

### How do I judge a major spend before I commit to it?

- What value could this create for customers or for the business?
- What will it cost in money, time, attention, and future commitments?
- What has to happen for the decision to work, and how likely is that?
- When will the business see a useful result?
- What else could the same resources achieve?
- What happens if the decision is wrong, and how easily can the business change course?

## 3. Grow the business without losing sight of long-term value

This area is about using outside money carefully when it becomes useful and making choices that build a valuable business over years.

### When is money from outside the business useful, and what will it cost?

- What exact problem would additional money solve?
- How much money is needed, when is it needed, and what will it be used for?
- When should growth come from customers, borrowing, grants, partners, or investors?
- What does the business give up when it accepts money from someone else?
- How do outside expectations change the choices a business makes?
- How can a founder keep enough decision-making power while the business grows?

### How do I build a business that becomes more valuable over time?

- What makes a business valuable beyond the money it earns this year?
- Which things create lasting value: customer relationships, product capability, data, reputation, systems, intellectual property, or the team?
- How do people decide what a business is worth?
- What can an owner eventually do with a valuable business?
- How do choices about spending, hiring, and funding today affect value later?

## Working rhythm

For each question, I will:

1. Choose the question and name the knowledge gap.
2. Read the relevant books and course material.
3. Build a first answer, model, or decision tool.
4. Apply it to a live case or realistic business example.
5. Present the work weekly and use feedback to identify what is still missing.
6. Revise the answer in the next cycle, or move to the next question when the gap is closed.

## First place to begin

Start with the first question: **How do I know a problem is worth solving, what it will cost to solve, and whether it is likely to work?**

That question creates the inputs for pricing, financial planning, and every later decision about where the business should spend its money and time.
