## FLAG 1: sources_vs_uses
Session: 1 | Date: 2026-07-17
Confirmed gap: Liabilities understood as **things the business spent money on** (uses) rather than
**claims by outsiders who supplied the money** (sources) — inverting the right-hand side of A = L + E.
What to test next: Give a transaction that changes the **shape** of assets without touching the right
side at all (cash → machinery). Ask what happens to liabilities and equity. The wrong model predicts
a change; the right one predicts neither moves. Follow with a borrowing, where the right side *does*
move, so the distinction can't be passed by guessing "nothing ever changes."
The rule:
- **Left side = what we have.** Uses. Cash, stock, machinery, receivables.
- **Right side = where it came from.** Sources. Only two exist: outsiders (liabilities) or the owner
  (equity, including profit the business earned).
- Same rupees, described twice. The two sides are **not independent quantities** — which is why the
  sheet balances by construction, not by effort.
- **Spending money never changes where the money came from.**
State at time of flag: ACTIVE (Priority 1 — stated confidently, unprompted, with no hedging)

## FLAG 2: asset_vs_expense_boundary
Session: 1 | Date: 2026-07-17
Confirmed gap: The asset/expense line drawn at **"did it produce value"** instead of **"do you still
control a resource afterward."** Predicts that buying machinery reduces equity. It does not.
What to test next: Two payments of the identical amount in one question — one buying equipment, one
paying rent. Ask which reduced equity and why. Identical rupees, opposite treatment; the "produced
value" model cannot separate them.
The rule:
- **Asset:** the resource still exists and you control it. Cash → machinery is an **asset swap**.
  Left side re-shapes, right side unmoved, equity unchanged.
- **Expense:** the resource is consumed. Nothing left to control. Equity falls.
- Downstream of FLAG 1 — the same sources/uses confusion in different clothing.
State at time of flag: ACTIVE (Priority 3)

## FLAG 3: debit_credit_forced_vs_arbitrary
Session: 1 | Date: 2026-07-17
Confirmed gap: "It's direction, it's conventions" — correct on direction, but **"convention" applied
to the whole system.** Blurs what is arbitrary (naming the left column "debit") with what is forced
(which side an account lives on).
What to test next: A **novel** account absent from the workbook's list of ten. Require the side to be
**derived** from A = L + E, with reasoning stated. Recall cannot pass this; the workbook's ten
memorized examples don't cover it.
The rule:
- **Arbitrary:** the word "debit" for the left column. Italian bookkeeping. Could have been anything.
- **Forced:** assets sit left because the equation puts them there. Not negotiable, not agreed.
- Filing the whole system under "convention" causes memorization of derivable facts — the exact
  failure mode of the rote-trained benchmark.
State at time of flag: FRAGILE
