Nobody actually wants "things easy"

You've thought about the day things get easier. 

Not now.... Eventually.

After enough leads closed, enough sales calls taken, enough people hired so you're not doing everything yourself. You picture the version of your life where you finally have room to breathe.

That's been the deal you've been running on

You know business is hard, but it's gonna be worth it. 

You keep putting in 5 extra hours on Fridays. It's 9pm and your phone has three unread texts asking where you are - You leave them on read.

You take the Saturday call because the deal was too good to miss. You build the SOPs at midnight so the team can handle it. You check the dashboard before you're out of bed.

More leads, more call --> more people to manage. The machine gets bigger.

But then you still wanna keep working more. 

Most people hear that and call it a problem- Workaholism, Avoidance. Something to fix.

They're diagnosing the wrong person

### **Three types of people**: The conquerer, the Merchant and the Explorer

There are three types of people who decide to build something.

Most people are a mix. But at the core, the operating system is one of three.

**The Explorer**

An explorer wakes up on a Tuesday and the first question she asks herself is what she actually wants to do.

The calendar is a suggestion . What does she actually want.

Maybe it's a two-hour walk through a part of the city she hasn't been. Maybe it's the Ethiopian restaurant she bookmarked three weeks ago. Maybe it's a coffee shop she found by accident where she stays until something interesting happens.

Her whole system runs on one logic: experience first, everything else second.

- **What she's after:** The freedom to choose. The ability to wake up and have the day be hers.
- **Why she works:** So she can stop working on command. And afford the things she loves to experience
- **What winning looks like:** being free to travel, to try out different experiences.

You can't actually explore until you can afford to. Freedom is downstream of competence. You have to earn the Tuesday first. Explorers tend to work incredibly hard. 

And in the start the need for explorations stops them from making faster progress in being more competent.

**The Merchant**

The Merchant works hard, sometimes way harder.

He wants the watch. Most times he's got no idea anything about the movement, the history, the craft. He wants the watch for the moment at dinner when she glances at his wrist XD 

Or when his old friend from school sees it and says nothing, but registers it.

He's working for an audience. Somewhere in the operating system there's a person he's trying to prove something to, A father.  Or maybe the version of himself at 14 who had nothing.

- **What he's after:** To be perceived a certain way.
- **Why he works:** Someone's watching, even if only in his head.
- **What winning looks like:** The moment someone else acknowledges it.

For a Merchant every deal closed is a point scored in a game where someone else holds the scoreboard. So the number never lands right. You buy the car and it doesn't do what you needed it to do. So you buy a bigger one.

The game doesn't end because you don't control when it ends.

I was a merchant when I was 16. 

I would buy a fancy earbud, a very fancy carbon-fibre-guitar when I couldn't play C major

**The Conqueror**

I was in 8th grade when I decided I was going to study maths.

I wanted to go beyond what I was being taught. I just knew it was the next thing I didn't understand, and that bothered me.

Three hours every evening on Khan Academy, I would study random ass shit-  Sinusoidal functions, limits, derivatives, functions et cetra.

I remember how disgusting sinusoidal functions were. I would stare at those curves looked like hieroglyphics.

My brain genuinely broke on this for two months on Sinusoidals. Two months of pain!

But then I finally understood the concept- after having to study basic trig first <. .> 

I ran through every problem I'd been stuck on and they were easy now, boring infact.

I finally would be looked at by my friends and teachers with respect. 

I thought I'd made it 

The moment I got recognition for math, I moved on to learning public speaking and hence I made my life miserable once again 

I would practice holding attention, commanding the room as my teachers would ask me to distribute notebooks. 

I got the highest grade for maths. It didn't even register. I just wanted to get better at my body language when speaking about history. 

- **What the Conqueror is after:** The next problem. Always.
- **What winning means:** The signal that it's time to find a harder game.
- **What stops them:** Only running out of walls.

The prize is never the point. The prize is just proof that this level is finished. The Conqueror doesn't stop at the top of the mountain to enjoy the view. They're already scanning for the next one while they're still climbing.


To be the explorer, the conqueror, the merchant, it comes with strengths and weaknesses 

The explorer has to learn freedom comes after you've earned it through incredible hard work on things sometimes you don't like

The merchant has to learn to do things for their own happiness, not to appease others

The Conqueror can't rest. They need to learn to experience 

### **3 Phases of Development:**
These are the three phases that the Conqueror, the Explorer, and the Merchant all go through 
1. Pain: You use the pain of not being where you are to work super hard in unhealthy ways to fuel your growth 
2. Love: then as you start getting results, you learn to love the work you do.
3. Purpose: then you realize this isn't about you. There is a calling and you start working even more.

**Pain**
For me, the pain started at the age of 12. I had no internet. I hadn't read any books. I couldn't cut sounds to make words, and I sucked at studies. I was fat af.

One afternoon I sat down and tried to picture my actual life at 25 if I kept going the way I was going. 

Wake up. Commute. A desk doing work I don't care about for a man whose approval I'd have to manage- Eight hours of that.

Go home to an apartment with no energy in it. A village where I'd have to explain myself to everyone, where nobody runs on the same frequency, where the closest thing to a tribe is people I have nothing real in common with.

A body getting slower every year until it starts limiting what I can actually do. 

The image became way more painful than the reality itself.

I had no love, no nothing to look up to. I realized I didn't "deserve" anything and where I was headed was a worse nightmare. So I just used the fear. Every day the awareness of what would happen if I didn't work was way more painful than the work itself.

I was miserable...

I would ask my school bus driver to drop me off a mile before home and ran the rest of it. Did that for three years until I could afford a gym. 

I begged my parents for a Kindle, told them it made monetary sense, and read a ridiculous number of books between April and December my first year.

At every moment I was afraid of what would happen if I didn't wake up at 5am. It felt like three muscular tigers were chasing me everywhere I went.

Pain is a horrible fuel. It burns dirty and it will take you to dark places. But when you're starting, you usually have a shit-metric-ton of it, so you use it.

**Love**

The tigers don't disappear. You just start running toward something instead of away from it.

Around 16 I got hired to teach public speaking. I remember standing in front of a room of adults older than me and realizing I actually knew how to do this. I loved the moment when something clicked for someone, when you could see the shift in how they were holding themselves. They'd lean towards you

I loved figuring out what was blocking them and then watching it move.

I tried engineering because I thought that's how you build things. Three months in, I dropped out. The building I wanted to do wasn't that kind of building that required me to solve abstract equations that helped no one. 

What I actually loved was figuring out what people want and what stops them from getting it. 

The gap between where someone is and where they could be.... That's business. That's what I love. 

The suffering it causes me, the long calls, the failed experiments, the months where nothing catches. I'm in love with all of it. 

Now things have been hard. I'm still early. I've been through my era of doing stupid shit that all of us do. Still doing it haha.

Love is more sustainable than pain because you stop needing external pressure to move. The work pulls you instead of the fear pushing you. And you can feel the difference.

**Purpose**

I'll be honest. I haven't reached this one yet.

I can see the shape of it. It's when you realize the work stopped being about you a while ago. 

There's something that needs to exist in the world that only you can build and you feel the weight of that. You're working because it would be wrong not to. From what I can tell, people who get here work the hardest of all. Because the fuel is almost inexhaustible.

And that's only possible when you're the only one that cares enough to solve a problem

### **Create your own problems**
The only constant in life is problems
>Natasha Romanoff: “Nothing lasts forever.”  
  Nick Fury: “Trouble, Ms. Romanoff. No matter who wins or loses

There are only problems. The question is whether you pick them or they pick you.

Most people let the problems accumulate around them. 

When I dropped out of engineering, I walked into a kind of darkness I hadn't accounted for.

My parents transformed into blood thirsty demons with two-red-horns. 

I was spending energy I didn't have trying to explain to people why I quit, why this made sense, while trying to figure out how to pay rent with nothing built yet. I was scared. 

I'd go on long walks listening to podcasts and research, absorbing everything I could, hoping it would make me feel less lost. I was doing stupid shit. The first few months were just eating dirt.

What most people do is look at that filthy dirt and step back. They try a few things, feel the discomfort, and go find something with less friction. 

Which is fine. But it means the arena belongs to whoever stayed.

This is where your archetype becomes important

> "Escape competition through authenticity." — Naval Ravikant

- The Explorer chooses problems that expand what's possible for her to experience. 
- The Merchant chooses problems that raise the stakes of the game he's playing. 
- The Conqueror chooses problems at the edge of what he can handle, because that edge is the only place he feels alive.

When you're solving problems aligned to your wiring, you get better faster because you're working with the grain of who you are. You don't run out of fuel in the same way.

There are only so few people willing to eat this much dirt- Genuinely few.

Someone playing your game because it looked good from the outside will tap out when it gets ugly. Someone who wandered in for the money leaves when the money is slow. But you, the person who chose this arena because it's the only game worth playing for you, you'll still be there when they're gone.

Go deep enough into the right problems and the question of competition stops being relevant. 


You're building something only you can build, because only you chose this exact set of problems and stayed with them long enough to understand them in a way nobody else does.
> "You can't compete with someone who's having fun." 

I'm still early. The experiments still cost more than they teach and the months are longer than I planned for and I'm still doing stupid shit I'll cringe at in two years, maybe even for more years XD

But I was 12 when the tigers started chasing me and I had no say in that. The fear picked me.

Now I pick the problems. I chose this arena knowing it was going to be dark, and it was, and it still is, and I'd do it again.

The desk I was afraid of at 25, the man whose approval I'd have to manage, the apartment with no energy in it. That future is still out there. I just stopped being the person headed toward it the moment I chose harder problems than the ones life was already lining up for me.

I haven't figured out purpose yet. I'm still mostly running on love and whatever's left of the fear. But the dirt is mine. I chose it.

That's the only thing that matters.

Pick the hardest problem in your arena. Solve it. Then pick a harder one.
