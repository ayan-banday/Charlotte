# How ICS Students Define Their Procrastination Problems

## Core Mechanism (Why Behavioral Prescriptions Don't Work)

**The trap is not avoidance. It's misdirected effort.**

These students are not lazy. They're not procrastinating in the classical sense (doing nothing). They're studying the wrong thing and calling it progress.

Why it's sticky: The meta-system (SIR schedule, mindmaps, learning articles, mental state) can always be improved. A retrieval attempt can expose a gap. So unconsciously, every time there's uncertainty, they redirect energy toward the unbreakable activity.

**They're working on the one thing that can never fail.**

**Why behavioral prescriptions fail:**
- "Stop optimizing, start retrieving" — sounds good for 48 hours, then they drift back because they haven't understood the mechanism
- "Readiness is a trap" — too abstract for this audience
- "You need willpower" — misses the point (they have willpower, it's misdirected)

**What works:**
Making them recognize their own specific flavor of misdirected effort. Thomsen reorganizing mindmaps for three hours. Kafka waiting to "recover the feeling." RyeBread spending six weeks setting up a marginal gains template. Same mechanism, different costume.

## Internal Monologue (What They Tell Themselves)

**The Readiness Trap**
- "I'll start once I feel ready / feel relevant / feel like my state is good enough"
- "I need to recover the right mental state before I can actually work"
- Student example: kafka (most explicit version)

**The Perfectionism Trap**
- "I need to get this right before I move forward"
- "I shouldn't move on until I've mastered this"
- "I've messed up the structure, let me reset and rebuild from scratch"
- Student examples: Thomsen, kafka, Kerberbos, RyeBread, majdoleen7309

**The Competence Gap**
- "I'm doing something wrong but I don't know what"
- Student examples: MilenaG, Thomsen, RyeBread

## What They're Actually Doing (The Misdirected Work)

**Meta-System Optimization (feels productive, blocks actual work)**
- Reorganizing mindmaps and optimizing small details instead of retrieving (Thomsen — very specific)
- Rebuilding their schedule or SIR system from scratch repeatedly (Thomsen, majdoleen7309, RyeBread)
- Switching between apps and task managers, reorganizing notes (Nirmal, RyeBread)

**Substitution Activities (feels like learning, not actual retrieval)**
- Going down research rabbit holes, reading articles, watching YouTube instead of applying (Thomsen, kafka, ADHD student)
- Doing practice questions as a substitute for actual retrieval/encoding work (MilenaG)

**State Recovery (feels responsible, is actually avoidance)**
- Sitting and trying to "recover a feeling" or mental state before allowing themselves to work (kafka — most extreme version)

## The Freeze Pattern (What Happens in the Moment)

1. Hit a moment of discomfort or confusion
2. Instead of pushing through: zoom out and start "fixing the system"
3. System-fixing feels productive but is actually avoidance
4. Once "fixed," something else is wrong
5. Cycle repeats

**Specific triggers for the freeze:**
- The schedule slipping even once (Thomsen, not_j2, socrates1.0)
- Getting interrupted mid-flow and losing the "feeling" (kafka)
- Comparing output to what they think it should look like (Kerberbos, socrates1.0)
- Hitting a concept or question they can't answer (not_j2, SiNisteRx)

## The Deeper Belief (Why It's Sticky)

**Core belief:** "I need to be in the right state to learn properly"
- Appears in 5-6 responses in different words
- kafka says it most explicitly
- Variants in: Thomsen, Nirmal, socrates1.0, RyeBread

**Secondary belief:** "If I do it wrong, I'll have to restart anyway"
- So they pre-emptively restart before even failing

## The Actual Cost (What They're Feeling Now, Not Later)

**Immediate emotional pain:**
- Embarrassment and shame that they've been at the same stage for months (Kerberbos: "I already feel dumb for taking so long")
- Gap between how hard they feel they're working and what they're actually producing (Nirmal, Thomsen, MilenaG)
- Isolation: feeling like they're the only one with this problem (socrates1.0, kafka)

**The cognitive dissonance:**
Working for hours feels good. Making progress doesn't. The two don't align.
