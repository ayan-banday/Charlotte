## Learning Loop Framework

**Four-part integrated learning cycle:**

1. **Get help from mentor.** Direct guidance, avoid dead-ends.
2. **Take real-world examples.** Study what works in the wild.
3. **Copy them.** Reverse-engineer the structure, not just the idea.
4. **Make them better.** Iterate on what you copied. Find gaps.
5. **Understand first principles.** Why does this work? What's the core mechanic?
6. **Try your own thing.** Apply the principles in your own way.
7. **Practice in real world.** Record actual webinar with actual people. Not simulation—real stakes.

**Why this works:** You're not stuck in theory. You're not copying blindly. You're cycling through mentor guidance, real examples, first principles, and live practice. Each loop tightens understanding.

## First Principles Before Building

Can't build anything (webinar, email sequence, product) without understanding first principles first.

Pattern: tried to build email sequence → didn't understand theory → output was garbage. Same with webinar building.

The sequence that actually works:
1. Understand what you're dealing with. What makes it work? What are examples of good? Study that.
2. Proceduralize actions. What are the steps?
3. Solve real problems. Real-world projects.
4. Build it out. But only after understanding.

**The principle:** You can't proceduralize what you don't understand. Understanding first, building second. Not the other way around.

**Recurring pattern:** Created sourcebooks to study webinars deeply. Identified the problem: "I don't understand what a webinar is." Solved it by creating resources. Then stopped and called it a day. Good preparation (sourcebooks created). Zero execution (didn't actually study). Building the board but not playing.

## Learning System: Books + Newsletters

Direct application learning. Lightweight system, maximum retention through use.

**The process:**
1. Pre-study. Light. Just a starting point. Best attempt at understanding before reading.
2. Don't actively build on pre-study. It's there, but don't engineer systems on top of it.
3. Read chapters. Direct engagement with content.
4. Think through application: How does this relate to my life? How do I apply this to the newsletter?

**What this is NOT:** Heavy note-taking systems. Building on top of study (meta-studying). Content organization beyond reading. Sourcebooks or elaborate frameworks (before reading).

**What it IS:** Read → relate → apply to newsletter. Lightweight, fast iteration. Direct connection to real work (newsletter writing).
