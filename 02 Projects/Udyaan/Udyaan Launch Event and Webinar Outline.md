# Udyaan Launch Event and Webinar Outline

## Event purpose

This is a one-hour launch event for the Udyaan student cohort. It should feel like a live conversation about becoming useful in the real world, not a series of formal speeches.

The event earns one decision: students should see Udyaan as the place to turn their discipline into visible proof of ability, then apply.

**Central line:** Your degree is the starting point. What you build, solve, and document becomes the proof.

## Webinar spine

### Opening: the student problem

**Owner:** Udyaan lead

Start with the shared fear across departments: students may graduate with knowledge, but no visible proof that they can contribute to a real problem. Use one short show-of-hands or response question to make the room participate before the first speaker.

Set the promise: by the end, students will understand why their discipline has a place in real work, why starting comes before confidence, and why Udyaan is a practical place to begin.

### Belief shift 1: Knowledge is not yet proof

**Owner:** Ankit

**Old belief:** If I study hard and earn a degree, people will automatically understand my value.

**New belief:** A degree shows what I studied. Real work shows what I can do.

**Job in the event:** Make the gap between classroom knowledge and demonstrated capability unmistakable. The audience should leave this section asking, “What proof do I have?”

### Belief shift 2: Every department can become a role

**Owner:** Vishal

**Old belief:** My department does not connect to professional or entrepreneurial work.

**New belief:** Every real problem needs different responsibilities. My discipline becomes useful when it is attached to one.

**Job in the event:** Make cross-disciplinary contribution tangible. Research, systems, finance, operations, design, communication, testing, and stakeholder understanding are all roles in a real venture.

### Belief shift 3: Entrepreneurial ability starts before the startup

**Owner:** Mansi

**Old belief:** I need a startup idea, a polished portfolio, or confidence before I can begin.

**New belief:** Entrepreneurial ability grows by noticing a problem, investigating it, taking responsibility, and acting.

**Job in the event:** Remove the identity barrier. This is not an invitation reserved for students who already call themselves entrepreneurs.

### Belief shift 4: Why agriculture?

**Owner:** Ritesh/ankit

**Old belief:** Agriculture is distant from me, so it is not a serious place for my skills or career to matter.

**New belief:** Agriculture contains urgent, human, commercial, and technical problems. It is exactly the kind of real-world environment where many disciplines can create measurable value.

**Job in the event:** Turn agriculture from a sector label into a proof ground. Fresh produce is lost through handling, transport, ripening, packaging, and poor information. Solving even one part needs research, material science, operations, finance, data, design, communication, and field investigation.

**Guardrail:** Do not make unverified impact or solution claims. Udyaan is still validating the G9 banana packaging problem and developing its solution. The credible message is that students can work on a live problem with real constraints, not that Udyaan has already solved it.

## Final Udyaan segment

**Owner:** Ayan

This is the final return and the only sell. It should follow this sequence:

1. **Show the real example.** G9 bananas can ripen quickly, suffer bruising and compression in transport, and lose value before they reach market. Udyaan is investigating how packaging can reduce these losses and build trust around produce quality.
2. **Connect the example to the four shifts.** This problem needs proof, roles across disciplines, action before confidence, and a serious real-world sector in which to learn.
3. **Reduce Udyaan to one sentence.** Udyaan gives students a place to work on a real problem, own a meaningful responsibility, and leave with evidence of what they contributed.
4. **Make the invitation.** Direct students to scan the QR code and register if they are curious, prepared to investigate, and ready to take responsibility.

The QR code appears only as part of this final segment. It is a close to a coherent argument, not a separate agenda item.
