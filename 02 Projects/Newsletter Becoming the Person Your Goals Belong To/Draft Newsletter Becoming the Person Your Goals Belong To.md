#### How I Stopped Being Sedated.
I started out thinking I'd love to be an engineer.

I moved out to study for engineering. Every single day I'd wake up at 5:00, shower, solve the hardest questions I could find, reflect, and get better. I'd get to my classes and answer some of the questions right. I'd feel like an alpha. But then I got a wake-up call.

The alarm clock rang with 80 mm mortars falling all around my home with a drizzle that would wake a gaint.

It was the first week of May and India and Pakistan had pleasantries exchanged over the borders- Operation Sindoor the indian side called it. It was retaliation at terrorist killing 26 innocent people. The whole nation mourned.

For me it wasn't just the sadness, but also pretty heavy bombs that kept me awake as sweat dripped off from my forehead.

I put on my noise-cancelling headphones and I would feel the vibrations of the motors falling in the vicinity. I wanted to pee my pants but fortunely I didn't have my bladder full. 

Every viberation of the ordance falling would shrink my balls by a few millimeters. 

My home is an army camp. They had S-300, S-400, and air defense guns- Love those russians:) When they'd launch counter missles/projectlies the ground would rumble, I could feel them eject out of their silos with a "woof."

I'd feel the ground shake and see lights streak across the sky as they tried to intercept the drones from the other side.

So I did what I always did: calculation.

The high-value targets that lay near me, the train station as a logistical hub to move in troops, the military targets, The probability of the shells falling in my neighborhood

I would hear the thud of the shells hitting the ground. Based on the light and the time to sound being heard, I would calculate how far away they were from my location. 

I would  put mattresses against my windows. I Packed my IDs, some cash, and water as a go-bag.

Every system I built was designed to protect one person.

Me

Then I walked into the other room. My sister, my mom, and my dad were all trembling. My mom had her hands pressed together. My sister hadn't moved from the corner not tryna move. My dad staring at the ceiling like he was trying to hear through it. He'd try to calm my mom and sister down.

I stood there with my go-bag and my calculations and I noticed how scared I was. But then looking at my sister the fear dissociated me from my body. I was afraid but I just suppressed it.

I put duct tape over their windows so the glass wouldn't become shrapnel. I ran the numbers on their position relative to the border, and the walls that surrounded the room. I told myself the hit probability was low.

And as I made those futile rationalizations under the thuds and "woofs" of the hot-potatoes being exchanged over the sky, I realized how selfish I'd been. 

My every protocol was desiged to protect me. 

Even if I became the best engineer in the world, I would still be a person whose first instinct in a crisis was to care for himself. All my systems, all my discipline, all my 5am mornings. They were all pointed inward.

That wasn't the person I wanted to be.

I didn't decide to leave engineering that night. But I realized what the path I'd chosen was leading me towards. And that wasn't the person I wanted to be. 

The sedation stopped working. The 5am routine, the questions I got right in class, the feeling of progress. None of it could drown out what I'd seen in that room.

Over the next few months I slowly checked out. Said goodbye to the engineering path. 

I lost so much aura with my fam and teachers in class.

But it was worth it. 

if I wasn't solving real people's problems than what was I doing? I wanted to understand people. I wanted to know how much pain they were in. I wanted to be a little less selfish.

Business became the way for me to go.

**Life doesn't balance, It seasons.**

- **Middle.** You're making progress. The excitement settles into momentum. You feel sane again. Balanced, almost.
- **Then it collapses back into limbo.**
