# VC Pitch Evidence Research

## Purpose

This is a research note for the discovery phase, not a claim that the current venture is ready to raise. It translates published investor guidance into an evidence standard for a future mini-pitch on a marketing-intelligence system.

The sources below are original material from venture firms. Their frameworks are useful lenses, not universal rules or proof that this opportunity is investable.

## What the investor sources say

### 1. The pitch is an argument, not a feature tour

**Source-backed guidance.** Sequoia's pitch framework asks for a crisp company purpose; the customer problem and current shortcomings; a unique, durable solution; why now; defined customer and market; direct and indirect alternatives; a plan to win; business model, team, and long-term vision. The implication is that market, alternatives, and the winning mechanism belong in the core story, rather than being appendix material. [Sequoia, *Writing a Business Plan*](https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan)

### 2. Evidence must match the company's actual maturity

**Source-backed guidance.** a16z partner Martin Casado distinguishes Team, Product, Repeatable Sale, and Unit Economics stages. For a pre-product company, he says the market, problem domain, and founder-market fit matter more than a polished solution. Once a product exists, customer quotes, exploratory pilots, and one-off services work are weak proof on their own; stronger signals include production use, engagement with real decision-makers, and customer diligence. A repeatable-sale claim needs similar buyers, similar use cases, and a growing pipeline. [a16z, *Aligning Startup Metrics with Stage of Maturity*](https://a16z.com/aligning-startup-metrics-with-stage-of-maturity-beyond-labels-for-fundraising-rounds/)

### 3. A crowded, urgent category needs a genuinely different customer experience

**Source-backed guidance.** Sequoia characterizes an urgent, crowded problem as a setting where being faster or cheaper is insufficient. The product must be meaningfully different for the customer. Its Wiz case study describes the useful shape of a differentiated insight: repeated customer interviews surface an unmet need, a specific technical approach removes the friction, and a short demonstration makes the difference tangible. [Sequoia, *The Arc Product-Market Fit Framework*](https://sequoiacap.com/article/pmf-framework)

### 4. Venture scale, differentiation, and founder fit are separate tests

**Source-backed guidance.** NFX describes its own early-stage filter as: can this become large enough to return a fund, does it have defensible and differentiated "magic," and is this the right team. Its illustrative floor is a plausible path to $100M revenue or more than $1B market capitalization, not merely a good small business. [NFX, *A Scientific Approach to VC*](https://www.nfx.com/post/scientists-view-vc)

### 5. “We collect data” is not a moat

**Source-backed guidance.** a16z cautions that data collection does not automatically create durable defensibility. A data advantage is more credible when data demonstrably improves the product, the source is scarce or costly to recreate, and customer trust or specialized preparation makes continued access hard to copy. It points to vertical product design, go-to-market, post-sales control, brand, and talent as other sources of defensibility. [a16z, *The Empty Promise of Data Moats*](https://a16z.com/the-empty-promise-of-data-moats/)

### 6. Expect claims to be checked outside the room

**Source-backed guidance.** First Round describes investment memos that cover the market/problem, product, vision, GTM, traction, team, and competitive landscape. It reports conducting 5–15 diligence conversations, often with prospective or actual customers, and advises founders to know their numbers and be ready for questions informed by customer and competitor research. [First Round Review, *What You Can Really Expect When Pitching Your Seed-Stage Startup at a VC Partner Meeting*](https://review.firstround.com/heres-what-you-can-really-expect-when-pitching-your-seed-stage-startup-at-a-vc-partner-meeting/)

## Reading this against the current project

### What is real now

- The current service wedge is the **Qualified Call Engine**: a 90-day optimization engagement for founder-led B2B agencies already running outbound, measured by qualified booked calls per qualified prospect contacted. It is intended to create a learning loop from ICP and offer through messages, objections, follow-up, and future tests. [[Offer Document]]
- The proposed product thesis is a persistent marketing-intelligence system that turns agency evidence and experiments into reusable growth decisions. The project explicitly treats this as a problem map, not proof of a wedge, willingness to pay, product, or venture case. [[01 Brain Dump for Venture-Scale Opportunity]]
- The discovery project itself says that a v1 decision scene, customer reconstruction, economic case, generic-AI comparison, and expansion bridge are still unproven. [[00 Introduction to Venture-Scale Opportunity]]

### What is unresolved or conflicting

- **[Inference from internal project notes]** The proposed ICP is not yet singular: the project introduction names 20–75-person performance-marketing agencies using HubSpot, while the offer begins with 5–20-person Indian B2B agencies. This is a research choice to resolve before presenting a single bottom-up market or repeatable-sales story.
- **[Inference from internal project notes]** The 2.25% qualified-call target is a service-performance target. It is not yet evidence that a software product can deliver the result repeatedly, at software margins, without high-touch intervention.
- **[Inference from sources + project notes]** “The system learns from campaigns” becomes a defensibility claim only if that learning produces a measurable advantage over a capable agency using its existing tools and generic AI, and if the underlying evidence is difficult to reproduce elsewhere.

## Evidence questions for the mini-pitch

These are project-specific inferences, not claims made by the sources.

| Proposition to earn | Evidence that would make it credible | Disproof / warning signal |
|---|---|---|
| One recurring decision scene exists | Reconstructed, recent customer workflows with the actor, trigger, current workaround, artifacts, decision, and measurable consequence | Interviews describe a broad wish list, or the decision is too rare for recurring use |
| The pain is economically material | Baseline conversion, downstream pipeline/revenue or margin impact, and an owner who can authorize a change | The harm is mostly founder annoyance or a metric with no financial or risk consequence |
| The insight is different | A causal explanation for failure that the buyer and alternatives miss, plus a testable product mechanism | The proposed product is a bundle of familiar dashboards, prompts, CRM fields, and advice |
| The wedge can win | A clear comparison with agencies, outbound tools, CRM/analytics, and generic AI on one chosen metric and guardrail | The buyer can recreate the result with their present stack and a short operating change |
| Learning is a moat, rather than a slogan | Evidence that the system improves recommendations/outcomes over time; rights to the data; structured labels; trust/access that competitors cannot cheaply obtain | Data is non-exclusive, unlabeled, rarely used, or does not improve the decision materially |
| The venture can expand | Named adjacent users, workflows, and budget owners that share the same evidence asset or decision engine | Expansion requires rebuilding the product, distribution, and data model from scratch for every segment |
| The team is unusually suited to win | A specific founder-market insight, privileged access, or execution advantage that is already observable | The pitch relies on generic ambition or an untested claim to understand the market |

## Practical pitch standard

**[Inference from the sources.]** A strong early mini-pitch should be able to state, in order:

1. The single decision that fails today, who makes it, and the monetary or risk consequence.
2. The non-obvious market insight explaining why existing tools and agencies do not solve it.
3. The narrow initial customer and why the problem is urgent now.
4. The wedge product or service mechanism, including its declared comparison against the generic-AI baseline.
5. The evidence collected so far, labelled honestly as **Fact**, **Inference**, **Hypothesis**, or **Story**.
6. The competitive alternative set and one believable reason the system can win the initial workflow.
7. The sequence by which the wedge becomes a larger, fund-return-scale business.
8. The defensibility mechanism and the milestones required before calling it a moat.

The immediate job is therefore to produce an evidence room, not to decorate a deck: interview reconstructions, source-backed market sizing, competitor comparisons, baseline metrics, experiment records, and a claim ledger. A presentation can then make only the strongest version of the truth currently earned.

## Research limitations

- These sources describe the perspectives of specific VC firms and partners. They should structure our questions, not substitute for direct customer evidence or a market verdict.
- This note does not contain market-size data, competitor research, customer interviews, or validation results. Those remain required before any venture-scale claim.
