# Problem Sourcebook v0.1

## Purpose

Create a single, evidence-labelled root for the current discovery work. This is not a product specification or a commitment to a wedge. Its job is to make the next customer conversations sharper.

## Working problem statement

[Hypothesis] Founder-led service firms struggle to turn customer, sales, campaign, and delivery evidence into repeatable growth decisions. The resulting learning is fragmented, slow, and founder-dependent.

## What is currently evidenced

- [Fact] Professor feedback on September 10 redirected the work toward validation with corporate, sales, and related operators.
- [Fact] Mario said the described business problem is happening within his company.
- [Fact] Ash reports two people in the target market segment have validated that the problem exists.
- [Fact] The detailed workflow, economic loss, existing artifacts, and buying process have not yet been captured.

## System map

1. Marketing, sales, onboarding/customer success, and product each produce fragments of customer truth.
2. Those fragments do not reliably alter the next offer, message, handoff, or experiment.
3. Decisions remain dependent on founder memory and ad hoc interpretation.
4. The firm loses time, revenue, margin, or customer trust at an as-yet unmeasured decision point.

## Candidate decision scene

[Hypothesis] A customer expectation breaks during a handoff between marketing, sales, onboarding/customer success, or product. A leader must decide how to classify the issue, what to promise, who owns the response, and what future process should change.

Unknowns: the actual buyer, trigger frequency, measurable loss, existing workaround, willingness to pay, and whether this is a narrow enough initial wedge.

## Why this is worth testing

- [Inference] If the decision recurs and the cost is material, a persistent evidence-and-experiment system could create value beyond generic AI assistance.
- [Hypothesis] The durable value lies in making learning attributable and reusable, rather than producing one-off recommendations.

## Discovery questions

Use reconstruction, not opinion polling.

1. Tell me about the most recent time this failure happened.
2. What happened immediately before it, and who noticed first?
3. What did each team do next? Where did information, ownership, or expectations break?
4. What did it cost in revenue, margin, time, risk, or customer trust?
5. What data, notes, CRM records, call recordings, or messages existed at the time?
6. What workaround did the team use? Why did it not become the normal system?
7. How often does this happen, and in what conditions?
8. Who would own a fix, and what would make it worth paying for?
9. What would a generic AI tool fail to do safely or reliably here?

## Immediate evidence plan

- Mario call, September 13: reconstruct one concrete incident and request the relevant artifacts or their descriptions.
- Follow up with the second validating operator using the same reconstruction guide.
- Use the answers to name one decision scene, one economic event, one metric, and one disproof condition.

## Disproof conditions

Pass or reframe the hypothesis if conversations show that the issue is too infrequent, lacks an identifiable economic consequence, is already solved by an existing workflow, or cannot be tied to a buyer with authority and budget.
