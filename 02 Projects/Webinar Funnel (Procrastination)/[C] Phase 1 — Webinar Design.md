# Phase 1: Webinar Design

**Objective:** Lock down the webinar strategy before building anything else.

The webinar is the core product. Everything downstream (email campaign, post-webinar sequence) depends on knowing exactly what the webinar is, what it promises, and how it closes.

---

## What This Phase Covers

Design the complete webinar architecture using the [[webinar-planning]] skill:

1. **7 Strategic Decisions**
   - Product name + one-sentence tagline
   - Price point
   - Target audience (who it's for + who it's NOT for)
   - 3 core objections (these become your 3 content sections)
   - Hook angle (the big promise)
   - Price anchors (high / medium / actual)
   - Guarantee

2. **Time Structure**
   - Intro: 10–15 min
   - Content: 40–60 min (3 sections, each destroying one objection)
   - Transition: 3–5 min
   - Pitch: 30–45 min
   - Total: 90–120 min

3. **Slide Outline**
   - Intro slides (target audience + exclusions)
   - Content slides (3 sections with proof + objection breaking)
   - Transition slides (recap + emotional bridge)
   - Pitch slides (offer + bonuses + guarantee + CTA)

## The BEFORE State (Student Pain & Mechanism)

**What They Tell Themselves (Internal Monologue):**
- "I need to get this right before I move forward"
- "I'll start once I feel ready / my state is good enough"
- "I've messed up the structure, let me rebuild from scratch"
- "I shouldn't move on until I've mastered this"

**What They're Actually Doing (Misdirected Work):**
- Reorganizing mindmaps for hours instead of retrieving
- Rebuilding their SIR schedule repeatedly
- Reading learning articles instead of applying techniques
- Trying to "recover the right mental state" before starting
- Switching between task managers and optimizing systems

**Why This Trap Is Sticky:**
All of this FEELS like ICS work. It FEELS productive. But it's not retrieval practice. It's meta-system optimization. The meta-system can always be improved. A retrieval attempt can expose gaps. So unconsciously, students redirect energy toward the unbreakable activity.

**The Freeze Pattern:**
1. Hit discomfort/confusion in real work
2. Zoom out to "fix the system"
3. System-fixing feels productive but is avoidance
4. Once "fixed," something else is wrong
5. Cycle repeats

**The Cost They're Already Feeling (Not Future Consequences):**
- Shame: "I've been at this stage for months"
- Gap: Working hard but producing nothing
- Isolation: Feeling like they're the only one stuck like this
- Cognitive dissonance: Hours of effort with no visible progress

**Data Source:** 13 student forum entries analyzing procrastination patterns. See [[[C] How Students Define Their Problems]] for full breakdown.
