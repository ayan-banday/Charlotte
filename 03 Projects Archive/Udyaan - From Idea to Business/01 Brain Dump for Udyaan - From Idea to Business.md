# Brain Dump - Udyaan: From Idea to Business

Raw thinking space for shaping the course. Promote stable decisions into the introduction file or a future curriculum blueprint when they are ready.

## Core Intention

The job of the course is to make Udyaan students business-capable. They should not merely understand business vocabulary. They should experience the movement from an idea to a real offer, a real customer interaction, a buying signal, a first build, and an iteration.

## Story Device

The student follows an original non-human adventurer mascot through a playful business world:

- Map the territory = choose and understand a market segment.
- Find the dragon = discover a valuable, recurring customer problem.
- Measure the dragon = understand urgency, cost, and existing alternatives.
- Forge the weapon = create an offer designed for the problem.
- Approach the dragon = conduct outreach and customer conversations.
- Test the weapon = present the offer and ask for money or commitment.
- Fight the first battle = deliver the smallest useful version.
- Study the battle = use feedback to change the offer, problem, customer, price, or solution.

The story must always map to a real business decision and a visible student artifact.

## Phase 1 - Map the Territory

Student problem: “I have an idea, but I do not know who it is for, whether the problem is real, or whether it is valuable enough to build around.”

Subphases:

1. Understand what a business is.
2. Choose a territory or market segment.
3. Find the dragon through customer conversations.
4. Measure the dragon's strength through urgency, cost, and alternatives.

Initial time estimate: approximately 3 hours per subphase, subject to validation.

## Phase 2 - Forge the Weapon

Student problem: “I understand the problem, but I do not know exactly what to sell, what result to promise, how to deliver it, or how to price it.”

Subphases:

1. Define the desired customer result.
2. Shape the value proposition.
3. Build the offer.
4. Prepare the sales and marketing kit.

## Phase 3 - Enter the Battle

Student problem: “I have an offer, but I do not know whether people will buy it, whether I can deliver it, or what needs to change.”

Subphases:

1. Find reachable prospects.
2. Approach them through outreach and conversations.
3. Test the offer for payment, pre-sale, deposit, or another meaningful buying signal.
4. Deliver the smallest useful version.
5. Study the result and iterate.

## Teaching Model

Each learning unit should follow this loop:

1. Written lesson introduces a concept or misconception.
2. Story beat shows the mascot facing a decision.
3. Loom video explains the idea in a human voice.
4. GPT helps the student practise, draft, role-play, or critique.
5. Student completes a real-world task.
6. Student submits a business artifact.
7. AI and human feedback improve the next version.

## Book and Framework Inputs

Potential sources include *$100M Offers*, *The Millionaire Fastlane*, customer discovery, lean experimentation, copywriting, and sales material. The books should be synthesised into decisions students need to make rather than presented as a detached reading list.

## Questions To Resolve

- What does “business-capable” mean at the minimum acceptable level?
- What counts as sufficient evidence of a real problem?
- What counts as a valid buying signal?
- Is one customer conversation enough for an early checkpoint, or should students complete three?
- How much of the final assessment is artifact quality versus real-world action?
- What does the GPT evaluate, and what requires human review?
- What happens when a student's idea fails?
- What is the minimum viable version of the course for the first cohort?

## Initial Time Estimate

Working assumption: approximately 3 hours per subphase, including lesson work, GPT practice, and the practical assignment.

Current subphase count:

- Phase 1: 4 subphases = 12 hours
- Phase 2: 4 subphases = 12 hours
- Phase 3: 5 subphases = 15 hours
- Total: 13 subphases = approximately 39 hours

At 90 minutes per day:

- Phase 1: 12 hours / 1.5 = 8 study days
- Full course: 39 hours / 1.5 = 26 study days
- At 5 study days per week: approximately 5.2 weeks

This is a learning-time estimate. Customer scheduling, waiting for replies, iteration, and mentor feedback may expand the calendar duration to roughly 6-7 weeks even if the active work remains close to 39 hours.
