# From Idea to Business

## Six-week course outline

A practical entrepreneurship course. Students take an untested idea to a real customer, a real offer, a real buying signal, and a first delivery, and they make money from it.

Agriculture is a valid market when that is the student’s territory. The outcomes stay the same in every market.

---

## Course promise

By the end of six weeks, the student can say: I picked a problem, I built an offer, I asked the market, I delivered, and I made money.

The workbook is the record. If it is not in the workbook, it did not happen.

---

## What the student can do by the end

1. Name a reachable customer and explain why those people can be reached this week.
2. State a problem in the customer’s language, with evidence from real conversation: what they already do, what it costs them, what is still a guess.
3. Write the before-and-after: where the customer is now, where they are after the offer works.
4. Write an offer: who it is for, what they get, what is included, how it is delivered, what they pay.
5. Run a demand test and read the result: paid, deposit, written commit, or a clear no.
6. Deliver the smallest useful version once and record what it took.
7. Keep an evidence file: decisions, conversations, numbers, and what changed. That file is the business plan.

---

## How each week works

**Open.** Text answers “What am I learning today?” and states the week’s outcome. Video appears only when a move is easier to see than to read.

**Do.** Fieldwork plus a GPT. The student pastes a prompt, puts in real notes, and answers questions. The GPT runs the drill. The market does the teaching.

**Capture.** Everything lands in the week’s workbook pages.

**Live session.** Students submit questions from the work. The session opens with a short webinar that answers those questions, then remaining live questions. Examples come from that week’s workbooks.

Optional reading for mindset sits in a bonus shelf. It is never required to pass the week.

Concepts in use:

- *The Mom Test* for conversations (their life and past behaviour, not “do you like this?”).
- *$100M Offers* for building the offer.
- *$100M Leads* for putting the offer in front of people and asking.
- *The Millionaire Fastlane* and *Unscripted* as optional mindset reading.

---

## What an offer is

Five lines, all filled:

1. Who it is for
2. What they get (the transformation)
3. What is included
4. How it is delivered
5. What they pay

If a line is blank, there is no offer yet.

Offer economics: if five people say yes, the student can still deliver lines 3 and 4 at line 5 without going broke.

## The six weeks

Each week has one transformation and one proof. Talking to people happens in more than one week. The job of the conversation changes.

| Week | Student starts here | Student ends here | Proof in the workbook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I have an idea, or I care about a space | This is the problem, this is who has it, this is the before and after | One page: who, problem in their words, A-to-B, a hypothesis that can fail |
| 2 | I have a problem | I have an offer, and I have started talking to the people it is for | Five-line offer, list of real people, notes from first conversations (understand, do not sell) |
| 3 | I think they want this | I asked. Here is what they did | Ask log: who, what was offered, ignore / no / maybe / yes / paid |
| 4 | The test is messy | This is the offer we are keeping, this is how much we can make, this is the smallest way to deliver | Recertified offer, reachable-money math, MVP steps |
| 5 | We have a plan | We are selling and filling the first jobs | Pipeline: contacts, live deals, money in or a dated commit; delivery started if someone paid |
| 6 | Something happened | I can show what happened, and it helps the next sale | Case study: customer, problem, what we did, result, money; complete evidence file |

### Week 1 — Problem and transformation

**Outcome.** A decided problem and a clear A-to-B.

**Proof.** One page. Named, reachable people. Problem in their words. Current workaround and cost where it showed up. Before and after in one sentence. One hypothesis that can be wrong.

**Conversations this week.** Understand. Last time this broke, what they did, what it cost. No pitch.

### Week 2 — Offer and first outreach

**Outcome.** A written offer and first conversations with the people it is for.

**Proof.** The five lines, complete. A short list of real people. Notes from those talks.

**Conversations this week.** Still understand. Map the customer. Do not ask for the sale yet.

### Week 3 — Demand test

**Outcome.** The offer left the building. The student asked. The market answered.

**Proof.** A log of every ask: who, where, what was offered, what they did.

**Conversations this week.** Demand. Same kind of people as week 1–2. The offer is on the table.

### Week 4 — Locked offer, money math, MVP plan

**Outcome.** The offer is recertified from the test. The student knows how much money this can make in a market they can actually reach. The smallest delivery is planned.

**Proof.**

- The five lines, updated from week 3.
- Opportunity in numbers: how many reachable people like the ones already spoken to, times price, minus cost to deliver. Count only people this student can reach.
- MVP plan: what the first paying person gets, the steps, the time, the cost.

### Week 5 — Sales and marketing

**Outcome.** Selling with the locked offer. First jobs filled.

**Proof.** Pipeline. Who was contacted. Who is in. Money received or a dated written commit. Delivery underway where someone paid.

This week is the demand test, run again, cleaner.

### Week 6 — Case studies and evidence

**Outcome.** A case study the next buyer, partner, or reviewer can read.

**Proof.** Customer, problem, what was done, result, money. Full evidence file. The file is the plan. A pitch deck is optional when someone needs to present.

---

## Passing a week

A week is complete when a third party can open the workbook and see the “ends here” column. Effort without that artifact is incomplete.

Week 6 aims at money and a case study of a real customer event. A documented refusal with a loaded next test is recorded as what happened. The target stays a paid result.

---

## What the course produces

A problem, an offer, a demand test, a first delivery, money, and a case study. The evidence file is the business plan. Agriculture is available as a market, not as a required identity. A certificate, if issued, sits on top of these artifacts.
