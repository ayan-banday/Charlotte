# Brain Dump: "Do Hard Things" (ICP1 — Week 2 of May)

## Core Insight
The false belief: **grind hard so things get easier.**
The truth: **grind hard so you can grind harder. The goal is upgrading your problems, not eliminating them.**

## Scale Economics & The Moat
- **What is it:** The more you do something, the easier replication becomes. Marginal cost drops.
- **How it connects:** When you do hard work at the peak, you build a moat. Not just because you reached the top. Because the more you climb, the more you learn how to climb. The knowledge compounds.
- **For Ethan:** He thinks reaching the peak means rest. Wrong. Reaching the peak means you can now see harder peaks. The knowledge he gained getting there becomes the spine. He uses it to climb higher. The moat isn't the mountain. It's the knowledge that makes the next mountain easier.

## The Core Spine Pattern
- Find something that works (one hard thing you can execute)
- Use that as the foundation
- Experiment around it (collect information)
- What emerges becomes what scales
- The marginal cost of doing the next thing drops because you already have the knowledge

## The Real Problem You Solve
- Most people do hard things once, then expect rest
- Ethan knows better. But he's still thinking "hard work leads to easier life"
- Truth: hard work leads to solving problems only you can solve
- When you're solving problems no one else can, that's when you can't be competed with and then you fall in love with the hard life


## Connection to Mark Manson
Upgrade your problems, don't eliminate them. Same idea. The work doesn't get lighter. It gets more valuable.

## The Chase is the Goal
Fall in love with doing hard things. What makes you happy isn't euphoria—it's meaningful, purposeful work. Chasing a dream.

The chase IS the goal until you can let go of the feeling of chase itself. That's enlightenment (another can of worms).

The implication: you don't solve for "reaching the top." You solve for loving the climb so much that reaching the top becomes irrelevant. You're already there.

## Identity Over Effort (Being vs Trying)

Stop thinking "I wanna figure this out" and move towards "I've already achieved it." Identity precedes action.

When you identify as someone who's already built the newsletter, your subconscious filters data differently. When listening to ideas on walks, your brain flags different information as relevant. The shift from trying to being removes internal conflict.

Core mechanism: Your identity acts as a filter. Being produces certainty. Trying produces doubt.

## Create Your Own Problems

Problems are inevitable. You either choose them deliberately or circumstances choose them for you.

Deliberately difficult circumstances force growth in ways comfort never will. Automation and innovation work this way: make hard decisions, accept short-term dips, build bedrock. Failed experiments aren't waste, they're the foundation.

For the newsletter: choosing the problem of teaching people to fall in love with hard things (not solve them) is the competitive edge.

---

## Choose Your Hard Problems

Choose which hard problems *you* want to solve. Then solve the hardest ones in that category, the ones only you can handle.

That specialization makes you unstoppable. The newsletter isn't about general productivity. It's specifically about the psychology of loving the work itself, not the outcome.

## Power Through Specificity & Counter-Positioning

**Source:** Seven Powers book, Counter-positioning chapter

**What is power in business:** Ability to keep solving problems (keep reducing friction, creating value). Competitive moat isn't walls. It's specificity. What you do is so tailored to you and so difficult that competition becomes irrelevant. Not because you exclude people, but because you're so far ahead they can't catch up.

**Counter-positioning strategy:** Do the exact opposite of what competitors do. This destroys their value and makes copying impossible.

**Example:** Others sell courses for $X. You sell the same course free (high quality). They can't copy because they're tied to revenue from courses. Now people trust you. Then you sell 1-on-1 implementation—solving "how do I actually apply this?" You charge more, get better conversion, faster word-of-mouth. They're stuck. You're exponential.

**Applied to "Do Hard Things" newsletter:**

Most entrepreneurs chase ease. Path: work hard → reach $40-50M net worth → relax. Done.

**Counter-position:** Choose hard things. Keep choosing hard. Love the hard things. As you do this authentically, you become successful in the metrics you care about (money, impact, whatever).

For readers (level 5 EgoDevelopment): they see the philosophy lived, not preached. They adopt it. They make more money because they're choosing to compete in the hardest spaces where moats are strongest.

**The moat:** Others are trying to relax. You're moving toward harder problems. They can't follow you there.

**For the newsletter:** The positioning isn't "productivity tips." It's "fall in love with problems others avoid." That's counter-positioned against every other productivity newsletter. Impossible to copy while maintaining your own brand.
