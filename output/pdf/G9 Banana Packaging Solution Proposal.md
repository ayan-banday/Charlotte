
## 1. Executive Summary

Post-harvest losses for G9 bananas in India range from 25-50% across the supply chain. Most of this loss is preventable through better packaging that addresses three specific failure modes: premature ripening from ethylene accumulation, moisture-related rot, and physical damage during handling.

We're proposing a low-complexity solution: a bio-plastic bag (PLA-PBAT blend) with integrated potassium permanganate sachets, perforated design for controlled airflow, and internal cushioning. This approach requires minimal manufacturing complexity while addressing all three failure modes simultaneously.

Expected impact: 3-5 days additional shelf life, 15-20% reduction in rejection rates, minimal cost increase vs. current packaging.

---

## 2. The Problem

### Why Current Packaging Fails

Bananas are shipped at 75-80% maturity in a pre-ripened state. The problem begins immediately after harvest. Current packaging in India uses cardboard boxes with kraft paper cushioning, LDPE liners, and flexible plastic tubes. These contain the fruit but don't control the environment inside the box.

Three things kill bananas in transit:

**Ethylene Accumulation:** Bananas produce ethylene gas, which triggers ripening. In a sealed or poorly ventilated container, ethylene builds up, accelerating the ripening process beyond what's acceptable by the time fruit reaches buyers. This ripening makes the peel soft, triggers brown spots, and reduces shelf life at retail.

**Moisture and Fungal Rot:** Bananas sweat during transit. Without proper airflow, moisture accumulates, creating conditions for fungal growth and skin rot. This appears as brown streaks and accelerates decay.

**Physical Damage:** Bruising and peel damage during stacking, loading, and unloading creates entry points for decay organisms and triggers accelerated ripening at damage sites. Current padding is minimal or non-existent in many shipments.

### The Numbers

India's post-harvest losses in banana supply chain:
- Retail level: 14.82% loss
- Ripening storage: 7.68% loss
- Wholesaler level: 5.90% loss
- Transport: 4.88% loss

For agri-exporters, this translates to:
- Shipments arriving overripe or in poor condition
- Forced discounts or rejection by international buyers
- Reduced market confidence
- Direct revenue loss

## 3. Solution Architecture

Our approach is built on a single principle: minimal change, maximum impact. Rather than designing a completely new container system, we modify the bag itself to passively control the microclimate.

**Three integrated components:**

1. Bio-plastic blend (PLA-PBAT) as the base material
2. Potassium permanganate (KMnO4) sachets for ethylene absorption
3. Perforated design with internal cushioning for airflow and protection

The blend is the container. The sachets handle ripening. The perforation and cushioning handle moisture and damage. Each component solves one problem. Together, they solve all three.

Why this works: We're not inventing new chemistry. Potassium permanganate is the proven, standard ethylene absorber in fresh produce globally. PLA-PBAT blends are already being used in India for food packaging. The only thing we're doing differently is structuring how they work together in one package.

## 4. Component Details

### 4.1 Bio-plastic Blend: PLA-PBAT

**Specification:**
- Material: Polylactic acid (PLA) + Polybutylene adipate terephthalate (PBAT) in 70:30 or 80:20 ratio
- Form: Pellets for extrusion or pre-manufactured film
- Thickness: 80-120 microns (depending on application)
- Finish: Matte or textured interior surface

**Why this material:**
- PLA provides rigidity and clarity (for product visibility)
- PBAT adds flexibility (so the bag doesn't crack or split during handling)
- The blend is fully compostable in industrial conditions within 45-90 days
- Both materials are food-contact safe and FDA-approved
- Available in India: Raghav Polymers, Bhagirath Industries, and other suppliers

**Sourcing in India:**
- Domestic suppliers: Raghav Polymers Group, Bhagirath Industries (world's third-largest bio-plastic manufacturer, based in India)
- Alternative: Import PLA-PBAT pellets (cost approximately 15-20% higher than virgin plastic, but competitive vs. traditional packaging when you factor in waste management)
- Minimum order quantities: Typically 500 kg for extruded film or bag production

**Cost estimate:** ₹80-120 per kg depending on blend ratio and volume

### 4.2 Ethylene Absorption: Potassium Permanganate Sachets

**Specification:**
- Active ingredient: Potassium permanganate (KMnO4)
- Carrier material: Activated alumina or silica (inert, food-safe)
- Sachet material: Non-woven polypropylene or breathable fabric
- Sachet size: 10-15 grams per unit
- Effectiveness: Reduces ethylene to 0.01 ppm (proven in lab conditions)

**Dosage:**
- 1 sachet per 5-10 kg of bananas (recommend 2-3 sachets per standard export box of 13-20 kg)
- Capacity: Each sachet absorbs ethylene for 10-14 days before saturation

**Placement:**
- Position sachets in upper corners of the bag, away from direct fruit contact
- KMnO4 is toxic if ingested; sachets must be sealed and kept separate from the fruit
- Use micro-perforations around the sachet to allow ethylene gas to reach the absorbent material

**Why sachets instead of embedding in the material:**
- Simpler to manufacture (no custom material synthesis required)
- Easier to replace if needed
- Allows reuse or modification without redesigning the entire bag
- Proven track record globally in fresh produce packaging

**Sourcing in India:**
- Potassium permanganate: Paras Chem (Mumbai), ₹175/kg in 50 kg drums
- Pre-made ethylene absorber sachets: Available from Fresh O2 and other suppliers on Amazon India and export packaging suppliers
- Alternative: Commission custom sachet production from packaging suppliers in Gujarat or Punjab

**Cost estimate:** ₹5-8 per sachet (if purchased pre-made); ₹2-3 if you source KMnO4 and assemble sachets in-house

### 4.3 Perforated Design for Controlled Airflow

**Specification:**
- Perforation pattern: Micro-perforations (1-2 mm diameter)
- Spacing: Maximum 25 mm between holes (complies with Indian APEDA export standards)
- Placement: Distributed across top third and sides of bag (not bottom, to prevent moisture pooling)
- Total perforation area: 5-8% of total surface

**Why this matters:**
- Allows excess moisture to escape without creating a fully open environment
- Maintains reduced oxygen conditions (slows ripening indirectly)
- Prevents anaerobic conditions that accelerate rot
- APEDA already mandates this for banana export packaging

**How it works:**
- Humid air inside the bag (from banana respiration) escapes slowly through perforations
- Ethylene gas can diffuse out gradually (absorbed by sachets before escape)
- Oxygen levels remain lower than ambient air, suppressing ripening without creating anaerobic stress

**Manufacturing:**
- Heat perforation (laser or mechanical punch) on PLA-PBAT film before bag sealing
- Can be done during the extrusion process (inline) or as a post-production step
- Equipment: Existing bag-making machinery with perforation attachment

### 4.4 Cushioning: Textured Interior or Foam Micro-layers

**Two options (test both):**

**Option A: Textured Interior**
- Method: Create micro-dimples or waffle texture on the interior surface of the PLA-PBAT film during extrusion
- Effect: Creates tiny air pockets that absorb impact without adding material thickness
- Cost: Minimal (design the extrusion dies differently)
- Advantage: Lightweight, integrates into the material itself

**Option B: Foam Micro-layers**
- Method: Bond a thin layer (0.5-1 mm) of expanded polyethylene or biodegradable foam to the interior of the PLA-PBAT film
- Effect: Provides direct cushioning against compression and stacking stress
- Cost: ₹5-10 per bag for the foam material
- Advantage: Proven protection, tested performance

**Recommendation:** Start with Option A (textured interior). If testing shows insufficient protection, add Option B.

**Why both options stay low-complexity:**
- No need for separate padding inserts
- Integrated into the bag structure itself
- Reduces handling steps and cost
- Works with existing high-speed bag-making equipment

## 5. How It Works

### The Mechanism

**Day 1-3 (In Transit):**
- Bananas are loaded into the perforated PLA-PBAT bag
- Ethylene is produced naturally by the fruit (this is normal ripening biology)
- Ethylene diffuses into the bag's interior
- The KMnO4 sachets oxidize ethylene to CO2 and water (chemical reaction)
- Excess moisture from fruit respiration escapes through the micro-perforations
- Cushioned interior protects against impact during loading and transport

**Day 4-7:**
- Ethylene levels stay suppressed (assuming sachet capacity is not exceeded)
- The fruit stays in the pre-ripened state longer than it would in a traditional bag
- Fungal growth is limited because moisture is controlled and bruises are protected
- Temperature remains stable due to insulation properties of the PLA-PBAT material

**At the Buyer's End:**
- Fruit arrives in pre-ripened condition instead of overripe
- Buyer can control ripening in their own chambers if needed (standard practice)
- Shelf life at retail is 3-5 days longer than current method
- Fewer brown spots, reduced bruising, better visual appeal

### Shelf-Life Extension Potential

Current situation: Bananas in standard packaging often arrive overripe or in poor condition after 7-10 days transit from India to export markets.

Expected outcome: Bananas remain in acceptable pre-ripened state for 10-14 days, giving exporters and buyers a 3-5 day buffer.

This translates to:
- Less rejection at international ports
- More time for ripening chambers to process fruit properly
- Reduced pressure on domestic wholesale and retail markets
- Better quality perception by end buyers

### Performance Assumptions

These estimates are based on:
- Published ethylene absorption rates for KMnO4-based systems
- Existing data on modified atmosphere packaging shelf life extension
- Indian and global research on banana ripening curves

We validate these in testing (see Section 6).

## 6. Testing and Validation

### What We Need to Measure

**Primary metrics:**
- Ethylene concentration inside the bag over time (Days 0, 3, 7, 10)
- Physical condition of fruit: peel color, firmness, visual damage
- Shelf life: Days until fruit reaches "unacceptable" threshold (as defined by buyer)
- Comparison: Bags with KMnO4 vs. without KMnO4 vs. current standard packaging

**Secondary metrics:**
- Moisture levels inside the bag (humidity sensor)
- Temperature stability during transit simulation
- Structural integrity of bag under stacking load
- Cost per unit vs. current packaging

### How We'll Measure It

**Phase 1: Lab Setup (Weeks 1-2)**
- Obtain G9 bananas at 75-80% maturity from a local supplier
- Pre-cool to 13-14°C (standard export temperature)
- Load test bags with uniform bunch sizes
- Place in controlled environment chamber (if available in your university labs)
- Install ethylene sensors (available from lab supply vendors)
- Track temperature and humidity with data loggers

**Phase 2: Parallel Comparison (Weeks 3-4)**
- Run 3 conditions simultaneously:
  - Condition A: New PLA-PBAT bag with KMnO4 sachets and perforations
  - Condition B: New PLA-PBAT bag without KMnO4 sachets (control for material effect)
  - Condition C: Current standard packaging (baseline)
- Measure at Days 0, 3, 7, 10, 14
- Photograph fruit in each condition
- Record any visible decay, fungal growth, or damage

**Phase 3: Field Transit Simulation (Week 4-5)**
- If possible, simulate actual transport conditions (temperature cycling, vibration, stacking)
- Or conduct actual shipment to a test buyer if timeline allows

### Success Criteria

We validate the solution if:
- Ethylene levels stay below 2 ppm inside the new bag (vs. 5+ ppm in current packaging)
- Fruit in the new bag shows no more browning/damage than current packaging at Day 7
- New bag extends unbruised shelf life by at least 3 days vs. current method
- Cost increase is no more than 8-12% vs. current packaging
- Bag is structurally sound under standard stacking loads (test with 5-6 bags stacked)
