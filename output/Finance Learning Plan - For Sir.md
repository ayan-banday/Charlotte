# Finance Learning Plan

## What I am trying to build

I want to understand finance through the decisions involved in building a real business, especially Udyaan and technology-enabled ventures.

The aim is simple: learn how a business earns, spends, protects, and uses money to grow without losing control of the basics.

## How I will learn it

I will work in short cycles. Each cycle starts with one real business question. I will learn the relevant ideas, apply them to a real or realistic case, make a small piece of work, and bring it for feedback.

The output matters more than completing chapters. It could be a pricing model, a cost comparison, a hiring decision, a cash plan, or a funding note.

## Core areas

| Area | Question I want to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Pricing and value | Why will a customer pay, and what margin is left after serving them? |
| Cash and profit | Is the business making money, and does it have enough cash now? |
| Financial modelling | What happens if price, costs, hiring, or customer growth changes? |
| Planning and runway | How long can the business operate, and when should spending change? |
| Major spending | Is this person, tool, research task, or purchase worth the money and time? |
| Funding | When should a business use customer revenue, debt, grants, partners, or investors? |
| Long-term value | What makes the business stronger and harder to replace over time? |

## First learning cycle

I will start with Udyaan or another live case and answer:

> What does it cost to create and deliver one useful solution, what could a customer pay, and what would make the work sustainable?

For this cycle, I will produce:

1. A simple list of costs: people, materials, travel, tools, and time.
2. A basic price and margin view.
3. A short note on the assumptions that need evidence.
4. A next-step recommendation: test, change, pause, or continue.

## What I would value from you

Your guidance would help me keep the learning tied to real decisions.

- Help me choose a practical business question each cycle.
- Point me to the right book, case, or example when I am missing a foundation.
- Challenge the assumptions behind my work.
- Tell me whether the output is useful enough to guide a real decision.

## Review rhythm

Once a week, I will share the question, what I made, what I learned, and what I will test next. The goal is to improve the quality of my decisions over time, not to rush through finance as a subject.
