# Finance Curriculum - Detailed Slide Notes

Use this as speaker notes or as a content brief for rebuilding the deck.

## Slide 1 - Learning finance through business decisions

**Purpose:** Frame finance as the discipline of using money, time, people, attention, and trust to create real value.

**Say:** I am learning finance through the decisions a growing technology-enabled business must make. A scalable business can reach more people without its costs rising at the same rate. The curriculum begins with questions that tell me whether an opportunity deserves more resources, then moves through planning, funding, and long-term value.

**Questions:** What resources are scarce? Which decisions make a business stronger over time? What kind of technology-enabled business am I trying to understand?

## Slide 2 - Pricing and value

**Purpose:** Connect what a customer gains with what the business needs to sustain itself.

**Say:** Price should begin with the change created for the customer. A solution may save time, reduce cost, increase revenue, reduce risk, or enable an outcome that was previously impossible. The business then needs enough money after delivery to serve customers, pay its team, improve the product, and survive mistakes.

**Learn:** Price is what the customer pays. Value is the improvement they receive. Cost is what the business gives up to create and deliver the solution. Margin is what remains after the direct cost of serving a customer.

**Questions:** What does the problem cost the customer today? Why pay rather than do nothing? What does it cost to win, serve, and retain one customer?

## Slide 3 - The next dollar and hour

**Purpose:** Make resource allocation the central operating decision.

**Say:** Most possible uses of money and time are useful. The job is to identify the best use of the next limited dollar or hour. If I spend 40 hours building a feature, those hours cannot also be used to speak to customers or close sales. If I buy a tool, it must save enough time or create enough value to justify the alternative uses of that money.

**Learn:** Opportunity cost is the value of the best alternative given up. Resource allocation is deciding where scarce resources should go.

**Questions:** What is the biggest constraint right now? What does this choice prevent? When should learning matter more than speed?

## Slide 4 - What is happening financially?

**Purpose:** Separate the basic financial facts needed for every serious decision.

**Say:** Money enters through sales, borrowing, grants, or investment. It leaves through product creation, salaries, tools, marketing, taxes, and repayments. Revenue is money earned from customers. Profit is what remains after costs over a period. Cash is money actually available now. A profitable business can still have a cash problem when customers have not paid, or money is tied up elsewhere.

**Learn:** Assets are useful things the business owns. Liabilities are money it owes. Cash flow is money moving in and out.

**Questions:** When will customers pay? Which costs quietly accumulate? How much cash can be used safely?

## Slide 5 - Building a financial model

**Purpose:** Show modelling as a practical way to test decisions before committing resources.

**Say:** A financial model links assumptions and calculations to possible outcomes. It can connect customer numbers, price, retention, cost of serving, team size, tools, and available cash. If price falls, it shows how many more customers are required. If hiring happens earlier, it shows how long cash lasts. It is not a prediction machine. It makes assumptions visible so they can be tested and improved.

**Learn:** Fixed costs stay broadly similar when sales change. Variable costs rise as more customers are served. A scenario is one possible future based on a set of assumptions.

**Questions:** Which assumptions matter most? What evidence replaces each guess? What changes when one input changes?

## Slide 6 - Financial planning

**Purpose:** Prepare for future conditions without pretending the future is certain.

**Say:** A useful financial plan has an expected, strong, and difficult version. It estimates money earned, money spent, and money needed. It reveals when hiring is affordable, when spending must slow, and when more cash may be needed. The difficult version asks what happens if customer growth is slow, costs rise, customer payments are delayed, or the economy weakens.

**Learn:** A budget plans expected income and spending. A forecast updates that plan from current information. Runway is how long the business can continue before cash runs out.

**Questions:** What must be true for expansion to make sense? Which expenses can reduce quickly? How often should the plan be updated?

## Slide 7 - Is the problem worth solving?

**Purpose:** Prevent expensive execution before evidence exists.

**Say:** First establish that the problem is painful and meaningful. Then estimate what it takes to create a solution: time, research, people, tools, money, and risk. Before building the full product, run the smallest credible test that can show whether customers care and whether the solution can work.

**Learn:** Validation is evidence that a problem matters and a solution has demand. Research and development is work that discovers, tests, and improves a capability.

**Questions:** Who has the problem? What does it currently cost them? What result would justify continuing, changing direction, or stopping?

## Slide 8 - People, tools, and research

**Purpose:** Apply the same spending logic to three common long-term commitments.

**Say:** A person costs more than salary: equipment, management, onboarding, and the work they replace or enable. A tool must save enough time, improve enough quality, or enable a result that would not otherwise happen. Research must clearly reduce an uncertainty, with a known point at which the next decision is made.

**Learn:** Compensation is the full package a person receives. Capacity is the useful work a person or team can complete. A recurring cost repeats regularly.

**Questions:** Hire, contract, automate, train, or wait? What determines fair pay? What evidence is research meant to buy?

## Slide 9 - Major spending decisions

**Purpose:** Give every large spend a repeatable decision test.

**Say:** A major spend is more than its price tag. Assess the value it could create, money and time required, chance of success, timing of results, best alternative, and what happens if it fails. A cheap decision can still be wasteful when it distracts the team. A costly decision can be rational when evidence is strong and the downside is survivable.

**Learn:** Expected value adjusts a possible result for how likely it is. Downside is the harm if it fails. Reversibility is how easily a decision can be changed.

## Slide 10 - Safety and growth

**Purpose:** Balance money held for safety with money invested in the future.

**Say:** The business needs enough cash for delays, mistakes, weak months, and surprises. It also needs to invest in learning and growth. Financial planning turns this tension into rules: what minimum cash stays protected, when hiring is allowed, and when spending slows.

**Learn:** A cash reserve is money kept for unexpected needs. Burn rate is how quickly a business spends more than it brings in. Liquidity means being able to access cash when needed.

**Questions:** What minimum reserve keeps us safe? Which costs can reduce quickly? How does a recession change the plan?

## Slide 11 - Funding and fundraising

**Purpose:** Treat raising money as a strategic decision with long-term consequences.

**Say:** Outside money is useful when it helps solve a specific constraint that cannot be handled safely from current resources. Customer revenue preserves control. Borrowing must be repaid. Grants come with limits. Partners influence direction. Investors exchange money for ownership and future influence. The business should know exactly what the money funds before raising it.

**Learn:** Bootstrapping means building with founder resources and customer income. Debt is borrowed money that must be repaid. Equity is ownership. Dilution means owning a smaller percentage after new ownership is issued.

**Questions:** What exact purpose does the money serve? How much is needed? What does it cost in repayment, ownership, control, or obligations?

## Slide 12 - Long-term business value

**Purpose:** Explain what makes a business valuable before an owner ever considers selling it.

**Say:** Long-term value comes from things that keep working and are difficult to replace: trusted customer relationships, a useful product, reliable systems, data, intellectual property, reputation, and a capable team. Current income matters, but a business becomes stronger when it can create value beyond the founder's constant involvement.

**Learn:** Recurring revenue is customer income that repeats predictably. Intellectual property is legally protected work such as software, designs, patents, or trademarks. An exit is a way an owner turns ownership into money, for example through a sale, merger, or public listing.

**Questions:** What makes this difficult to copy? Which assets are everyday decisions building? What makes income reliable rather than one-off?

## Slide 13 - Weekly learning loop

**Purpose:** Make progress measurable through work, feedback, and improved decisions.

**Say:** Every cycle begins with one business question. I read the relevant material, build an answer or model, apply it to a real or realistic case, present it each week, receive feedback, and improve the next version. The evidence is the work produced: a pricing model, a hiring decision, a spending comparison, or a funding analysis.

**Questions:** What did I build or test? Which assumption did I test? What did reality show? What changed after feedback? Which question comes next?
